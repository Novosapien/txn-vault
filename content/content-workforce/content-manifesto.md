# TXN — Content Manifesto

> **Section:** [[content-workforce]]
> **Status:** **Complete through every section**, closed 21 August and loaded into the platform. Wording passes remain and they are Tyler's, not the client's.
> **Last worked:** Session 2, 21 August 2026 ([[2026-08-21-content-workforce-interview-2]])

The manifesto answers what the business stands for, what it would write about continuously, and who it is writing for. The audience is the load-bearing part.

## Where the North Star landed

Several passes were worked through in session 1. The direction that held:

**The barrier to entry is the thing being removed.** Launching a card programme is complex enough that businesses which could benefit from one defer it, or drop it below the line in annual prioritisation. Lowering that barrier means more programmes get launched.

Two refinements mattered:

- **Knowledge is the driver, not the outcome.** Removing the need for card expertise is the mechanism. The outcome that matters is **less time and less effort** to launch and run a programme. A version that removed the expertise requirement without reducing time and effort would be a failure.
- **Do not position against the specialist.** Wording that implied removing the expert was rejected: it alienates businesses that already employ card specialists, and the consultants who refer work. The agreed framing is **removing friction**, which says the same thing without making an enemy of the expertise.

## Audience segmentation agreed

Two distinct customer categories, requiring different messaging:

| Category | Description | What they need to hear |
|----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Card is a prerequisite** | The core business demands a card product, for example a digital bank without a debit card is not much of a bank | The decision is already made, so the message is about making it as easy as possible with the lowest effort |
| **Card is additive** | The core business does not require a card, for example a trading platform adding one to deepen the customer relationship or open a revenue line | The message must remove the barrier to entry, so a card programme survives annual prioritisation instead of landing in the too-difficult bucket |

## Positioning decisions

- **Expertise belongs in the product**, not behind tickets, calls and weekly project meetings. Where knowledge is only reachable by raising a request and waiting on a human at the vendor, the customer's progress stalls.
- **Guidance while doing the task**, not before it. The customer should not have to read documentation to acquire understanding first; they should gain it in the flow of the work.
- **Self-serve is the mechanism.** Every loop where a customer waits on a vendor response is time lost, and integration is where this bites hardest.
- **Expertise is real and expensive, and should be built once into the product** rather than re-hired by every customer.

## Section decisions, session 2 (21 August)

The draft was walked section by section. Ian led; Dorte carried specific wording. Ten decisions, all recorded because they are the guardrails the workforce writes inside.

### Central conflict

- **Bridge the two audience categories, do not split them.** Ian rejected the framing that a business which must have a card "hires the expert it never wanted". Those organisations usually do employ payments people; they are simply not card specialists. The value is **limiting how much card-specific expertise is needed from day one**. A version A and a version B of every persona is explicitly to be avoided.
- **Cut the AI-versus-human line.** *"Capability without comprehension"* went with it. Ian: *"there's no value to having that in the manifesto."*

### Vision of the future

- **Lead with the bolder claim.** "We propose, you approve" comes before "you drive, we advise". The first is TXN identifying improvements from the data it holds; the second is the client doing the thinking and TXN reporting impact.
- **Agent-to-agent is one model of three.** The other two are building straight to the APIs, and using the console with or without the co-pilot. The draft read as though agent-to-agent was the only route, which contradicts meeting the client where they are.
- **Cut "blast radius"**, and cut the first sentence of point one as redundant.

### Core promise

Accepted broadly. **Cut "you do not arrive expert"**, which neither Ian nor Dorte could parse.

### Target audience

Three personas generated, up from the usual two, and accepted. Two corrections:

- The technical persona is **not the decision maker but a significant influencer**, and is never simply handed a vendor and a deadline. Ian has never had a client where the integrator sat outside the evaluation.
- Their fear is not only their own workload. It is **choosing a platform their team will hate working with**, developer tools and APIs included, because that puts friction between the leader and the team.

### Core tenets

- Points 1, 2 and 4 stand.
- **Point 3 becomes an earned-trust model**, and Ian endorsed the rewrite. Permissions widen as trust is built. *"Suspending one card and terminating 400 must never share a permission model"* is true on day one and may not be true forever. Brett's phrase, **"an earned trust model"**, is the one Ian took.
- **Points 5 and 7 cut.** Five repeated the first four; seven argued against a position nobody holds.
- **Point 6 kept**: built AI-native rather than retrofitted. Dorte queried the console example, not the point.

### Stakes and call to arms

- **Do not disparage "AI added to consoles built for specialists."** TXN does exactly that with its own co-pilot. Nothing in the manifesto may make any of the three interaction models sound inferior.
- **Cut the attributed quote from Ian** about moving fast and staying ahead. It is an internal position and of no external interest. The position that replaces it: TXN will **push the boundary of the category but never ship unproven AI capability**, because capability that fails operationally *"would be pretty catastrophic for us"*.

### Style

**No em dashes** in generated content, instructed live into the engine.

## Open

- Whether TXN positions as a **disruptor** or as **evolution**, which sets how bold the language across all four entities can be. Raised in session 1, **not settled in session 2**, and it still gates how bold each individual entity may be.
- Wording throughout. Ian repeatedly declined to word-smith and routed it to Tyler: *"I'm trying to pull back from worrying about the language too much because I'm sure that Tyler can get into that."* That is the right split and it makes the language pass a Novosapien task, not a TXN one.

## Working note

The manifesto is an **authoring input, not published copy**. It shapes what the workforce writes; it is not intended to appear on the website in this form.
