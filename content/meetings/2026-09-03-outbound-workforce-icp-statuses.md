## The ruling: four statuses, in priority order

### How three became four

The vault's model going in was **three routes** (no incumbent / next product elsewhere / full switch), established on 25 August. Ian broke it in his first substantive comment:

> *"I can see the first one um no incumbent next product elsewhere full switch. I don't see how that can be true. How how can you have a full switch or the next product elsewhere without an incumbent?"*

Restating it cleanly, he produced three groups:

1. Has a card program, therefore has an incumbent, looking at either a **full switch** or **launching the next product with somebody else**
2. **No card program yet**, needs to launch one **as part of its core product**
3. **No card program**, in an industry where peers have launched programs against use cases that could apply here

Then he split the first, because the two halves are not one motion:

> *"it is distinctly different carrying out a full switch versus launching a new car[d] product with with the incumbent remaining in place."*

Brett: *"different service, different offering, different approach, different migration. There's a different everything, different business model they're addressing, different rationale as to why they're buying it."*

Ian's clinching reason is the qualification questions, not the product:

> *"there's a whole different set of key questions that they will have in qualifying TXN which are very heavily focused on the risk of migrating card holder data from one provider to another which they don't have if they're just launching a new card program and keeping the incumbent in place."*

### Not ICPs, statuses

Ian was careful about what he had just produced, and explicitly relaxed about the label:

> *"I'm not even sure it's I don't even know if it's four ICPS. I just think it's four statuses almost of where a company is in launching a card program, where we might find them. I'm not too concerned about the terminology whether it's ICP or not."*

And on where it sits in the framework:

> *"I consider this to be the the very top level of one of the ICP parameters... I don't think it's the determining the single determining factor, but I think it's at the top level."*

That sentence is why [[icp-definition]] gives Card Program Status the largest single weight and **no gate**. Top-level, not determining.

### The order, and the reasoning

Ian ranked them without hesitation, then justified each:

| Rank | Status | Ian's reason |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **1** | **First program, card is core** (S3) | *"you're 100% sure that they're going to do it. The only way they don't do it, Brett, is if the company just doesn't exist."* Totally greenfield, and it is the case TXN's proposition is built for: helping customers launch without card expertise |
| **2** | **New program, incumbent stays** (S2) | *"they might change their mind about launching a new program and it makes no difference because they've already got one."* Discounted for reversibility, not for fit |
| **3** | **Industry inference** (S4) | *"there's a period of convincing in the value of the card program before you get into [why] TXN. So, it's a somewhat different sale."* In the first two, the decision to launch is already made and the conversation is vendor selection |
| **4** | **Full switch** (S1) | Reputation and tooling, below |

Brett had run the same exercise privately and landed identically: *"Where's the least risk? The least gates that you need to get through."*

### Why full switch is last, and it is two reasons not one

**Reputation.** Dorte: *"we need to build up the reputation before anyone would switch their full [book]."*

**Cost of pursuit.** Ian's comparison is the sharpest thing in the session:

> *"I think full switch falls into the same category as going after a tier one bank for me. The resource effort it takes up in trying to win it means that you don't have... you take your eye off the ball on the other ones that are more green field that you've got a higher percentage likelihood of being able to a win and b service."*

So the full-switch deprioritisation is **the same rule as the tier 1 bank anti-profile**, applied to a status rather than a company type. Both say: the pursuit cost is the disqualifier, not the fit.

Brett added the capacity half: *"you've got limited sales capacity in the early stages. You don't need to be drowning in huge migration analysis and business analysis."*

**And a third gate that is not commercial at all.** Ian: *"we also have to build some tools to make it easier. People will expect that. They won't expect the old 15 year old approach to migration."* **TXN has no migration tooling**, and S1 needs it. That is a product gap sitting behind a GTM ranking, and it is the reason S1 cannot simply be reprioritised later by choice. Raised at [[open-questions]] #78.

## The problem Ian raised and Brett parked

The most important unresolved thing in the session, and it is not in [[icp-definition]] because it was deliberately held back.

Ian, having settled the statuses, turned to what sits beneath them:

> *"when I use the expression industry verticals, I'm it's a bit of a mishmash of an a true industry vertical and really a use case. So when you say digital banking or expense management, they're not really verticals, they're use cases."*

Each status has a **one-to-many relationship** with those use cases, so the mishmash propagates.

His worked contrast:

- **Investment and wealth management** is a genuine industry vertical, and the card inside it *"is pretty... it's just a bog standard payment card, there's nothing special about it."*
- **Expense management** is not a vertical at all: *"the only people who offer expense management are people who that that's their core business."* The use case **is** the company.

So a card offered by a trading platform *"looks very similar to one that's offered as part of a digital banking offering or... a travel card type thing."*

> *"We are going to need to get clarity on what constitutes being an industry vertical and what is a use case."*

**Brett banked it rather than feeding it in:** *"I'm not going to put this in. Hold on. Let me just make sure I don't put it in. I'm just going to pop it here. That's good insight. I'm going to bank that insight... I think we're on the right path. Let's see where it lands and we can bring that back in if we need to do some shifting."*

That was a reasonable call in the moment, since the session was finally running well and the point would have derailed it. **The risk is that banked insights are not written down anywhere.** It is not in the rewritten ICP, whose §4.2 still lists both kinds of thing in one Sub-sector list. Recorded at [[open-questions]] #79.

### The related boundary Ian drew

He also ruled out a whole category of work while explaining this, and it is a useful scope line:

> *"it's not that we looked at that we're going to look at different industry verticals and say, 'Ah, do you know what? They could be a good bet for having a card program.' Because that's really right at the start of the thing that we don't have the capacity to do it... That's trying to create your own proposition for an industry that you're not actually in and then go and test it. You know, that's not what we're here to do."*

**TXN is not inventing new card use cases in untouched verticals.** The verticals on the list are there because programs are known to exist in them. That bounds S4 (industry inference) to verticals with observed precedent, and it bounds the discovery research too.

## What else was settled

### Card-essential versus card-adjacent

The test is Ian's own, and it is a good one:

> *"I would say banking, digital banking, expense management, and crypto are the ones that if you went without a card, your product would look odd. The others are optional."*

Crypto qualifies on a specific mechanism: *"that's an offer for people to be able to convert from crypto to fiat and then spend."* Everything else has *"demonstrable use cases where a card program is there... but it's not necessarily core."*

Ian also corrected the framing on S3 itself: *"I don't think it's fair to say the card **is** the product. I think it's more the card is **essential to** the product."*

### Employee count must not drive the ranking

Ian's reasoning is that headcount and card-readiness are uncorrelated in both directions. A scaled business may have built everything except the card and *"now they've arrived at the point where they need card"*, with ten employees. A startup may build cards from day one with fewer than ten.

> *"the number of employees to a certain degree it's relevant but it doesn't warrant a significant score."*

And the operational reason it must not gate: TXN needs to monitor incubators *"for startups who are not announcing themselves, but we know what kind of company they are and we can determine whether or not a card program is going to be core to their product."*

Brett's instruction to the model, verbatim: *"for employee band it is not a priority because there [are] too many different size businesses that could capitalize on this. Don't use this as a core ranking."*

### Never criticise the competition

Raised on S2, where naming what the incumbent cannot do is most tempting. Brett offered the choice:

> *"do you want to go, hey, this is why you should use TXN, and by the way, your incumbent is [poor]? Or do you want to be going, hey, this is what TXN does, but highlight the benefits that the incumbent most likely doesn't have?"*

Ian: *"it's the latter. Like what I might say to somebody over a pint is very different to what I'd say in an outbound sales campaign or any content that gets published anywhere."*

Asked whether the same restraint applies to S1, where the prospect is actively leaving someone:

> *"The answer to that question is yes. I just don't believe in criticizing the competition. It's more important to emphasize all differentiators as you understand them."*

**So it is binding in every status, not only where an incumbent stays.** Brett's recorded instruction: *"we never want to go after an incumbent. However, we do want to position the TXN benefits or USPs that the incumbent currently does not have. We've got to be subtle about this."*

### The limited-capital anti-signal comes out

Ian went through the anti-profile and found one item that cannot be operated:

> *"I would question its inclusion on the basis that in determining who to target, how would you know?"*

> *"they can talk about what they've raised as much as they like, but you don't necessarily know how much of that is being used and directed towards [this]. You don't know what their plan is for the next capital raise... it's almost impossible. I think we could just get stuck in an endless loop of trying to analyze data that we really still won't know the story on."*

**With that removed, the anti-profile is identical across all four statuses.** Ian confirmed it explicitly: *"if you remove the thing regarding capital out of the anti-signals completely, then yes, all of the anti-signals are the same across all of the statuses."*

### Light-touch tracking for the undecided

On the band covering companies with no decision yet, Brett: *"you need that in there. We need to be tracking so that at least you start establishing conversations for when they do decide."* And the constraint in the same breath: *"That's got to be light touch."* Ian agreed.

Brett named the difficulty honestly: *"this is the problem, this observability, trying to somehow figure out how we're going to keep an eye on it"*, then reframed it as presence rather than surveillance, *"it's more in being in your face than keeping an eye on it."*

Ian owns the check. Brett deferred it rather than block: *"I'm going to leave it for now. We can add it in later."*

**Why it matters more than a to-do.** These are claims an outbound agent will make, and the honest form of the claim is *the platform is operated by an accredited partner*, not *TXN is accredited*. That is exactly the ground [[open-questions]] #66 covers on platform provenance, and the same distinction Ian drew on 27 August between the platform and the environment it is operated in. Raised at [[open-questions]] #80.

**Dorte caught the defect immediately:** *"I've just seen that you had a bank as a priority one. I don't think we will land a bank to start this."*

Brett's answer is the right one and it is also the point: *"Remember, we haven't fed any of the new information that we've done."* The run predates every ruling from 2 and 3 September, so it is scored on the retired framework.

Brett has committed to a rerun, *"I'll rerun the register correctly... this is something I'll run in parallel at a later time."*

Dorte's presentational note, worth acting on: the artifact is dense and needs graphs to break it up, *"because it's so much... it breaks it up and makes it easier to go back and forth."*

## Where the first deal comes from

A short exchange that settles something the vault has carried loosely.

Brett: the first deployment matters most, and the effort should go into *"trying to also open up your existing network. So your LinkedIn profiles know of you. They're going to be interested in what you're saying. I think [that] will probably be the one that's going to land you the most likely opportunities, because you've already established that trust within that network rather than completely cold."*

Ian agreed, and was candid about why the engagement exists at all:

> *"Neither none of us are prolific posters of content on LinkedIn. Frankly, it's very low on the list of things that I have time to do. Hence the engagement with you guys."*

> *"from a direct point of view, I tend to agree that seems to feel like it's probably the most likely. And then obviously the two channels that we talked about yesterday, primarily the schemes and the bin sponsors."*

**So the ranked routes to a first client are: the team's own networks, then schemes and BIN sponsors, then cold outbound.** That is consistent with [[commercial]], which already sequences outbound behind the launch, and it puts the Content Workforce ahead of the Outbound Workforce in expected value for the first deal. Both engagements are running in parallel; only one of them is amplifying a network that already exists.

## Ian's parallel work, and an incoming input

> *"why have businesses in that sector set to launch card programs, what is in it for them, why have they done it, and then also the second part being why choose TXN. Two-layered sale: this is why you should launch a card program, this is why you should do it with us."*

He will send it, with a deliberate caution: *"I think you need to read [it] before you decide whether to feed it in... **Commercially material:** the reason he is doing it is *"we've got some potential referrals or introductions within the investment and wealth management vertical."* That is a warm route in a named vertical, and it is the first one recorded on this engagement.
