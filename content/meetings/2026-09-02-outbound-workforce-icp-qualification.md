# TXN: Outbound Workforce, Additional Interview (2026-09-02)

> **Source:** Gemini transcript, 2 September 2026, 09:59 BST, 1h 20m. Brett StClair with **Ian Johnson** and **Dorte Dye**. Continues [[2026-08-24-outbound-workforce-interview-1-offer]] and [[2026-08-27-outbound-workforce-offer-session-2]]; the ICP material continues the 25 August interview 2 held on [[outbound]].
>

It ran live against the model's own confirmation questions with the screen shared. It **did not finish**. The session degraded in its final twenty minutes, and Brett ended it to restart in a fresh context window. What did land is a set of rulings on qualification that are more consequential than the agenda implied.

## The headline: three gating rules overturned

Ian was reading back the disqualification bands in [[icp-definition]] v0.4 and did not recognise the behaviour they produce. Three of them changed in the space of twenty minutes.

### 1. "No card program and no stated plan to launch one" no longer disqualifies

This is §8.1.8, parameter P7, the Incumbent Processor Signal DQ band. Ian's objection was immediate and concrete:

> *"if it's disqualifying no car[d] program and no stated plan to launch one, then what it does is disqualify things like trading platforms, which fall into that conversation."*

His worked example is the sharpest thing in the session. In the trading platform vertical, **Trading 212** and **Trade Republic** have both launched successful programs with Marqeta. A whole raft of companies in that same sector have not. Those companies are not out of scope; they are the ones who have not been sold to yet.

> *"it can't be a disqualifier that there's no signal that somebody is planning to launch a car[d] program. If we give a set of verticals or sectors that we think we should target, it's because there are use cases within those verticals or sectors that are there today already."*

And the reason absence of signal cannot be read as absence of opportunity:

> *"green field companies often there are no public signals. That was the point I was making about the first point. How would we know? There's a could be a bunch of people planning to launch a car[d] program. We have absolutely no idea they're going to do it, but they fit squarely in verticals that we believe are use cases that are applicable."*

> *"where they are right now in that decision making process is not where they might be in six or 12 months time. So when they do decide or if they decide to launch a car[d] program, we want to be right there front and center."*

Brett agreed and named the commercial reason: *"your market's so small, you want to identify these and keep it as open as you can and keep those nets a bit more open."*

### 2. Regulatory licence status no longer gates

Asked directly whether regulatory licence status still gates, Ian answered in one word: **"No."**

[[icp-definition]] §8.2 currently names Regulatory Licence Status (§8.1.5) as one of **four critical parameters** whose DQ band disqualifies the account outright, alongside Sub-sector, Geography and Triggers. That is now wrong.

### 3. The incumbent-contract DQ is a scheduling rule, not a disqualification

§8.1.7, the Behavioural and Situational Triggers DQ band, reads *"recently signed a multi-year incumbent contract within the last 12 months with no offsetting trigger."* Triggers is also a gating parameter, so today that band disqualifies. Ian did not recognise it as a disqualifier at all:

> *"I don't recognize the DQ band of recently signed a multi-year incumbent contract within the last 12 months with no offsetting trigger. I recognize if somebody... has signed a a contract with an incumbent within the last 12 months, I would class that as being a disqualifier from a priority perspective. That's more of a scheduleuling [scheduling] then Brett which is there's no point doing anything with these guys for you know the next 24 months."*

> *"So don't disqualify them but move them down the list so that they're scheduled at a more appropriate time."*

He also picked up §8.1.10, the scheme and BIN sponsor DQ band, on the same grounds: *"again, it's not necessary that we would know that's the case."* That band is not gating, so it costs points rather than the account, but the reasoning is identical.

### What this confirms

**This is the client independently reaching the finding [[qualification-matrix]] recorded on 25 August.** That analysis said the scoring framework is structurally a measure of card program maturity, which is right for fit and a poor proxy for near-term winnability, because maturity implies an incumbent and an incumbent implies the track record objection. Ian arrived at the same place from his own side, without reference to it, and his diagnosis of the model's behaviour is exact:

> *"I found that that Claude was bit was trying to narrow things down to more of a prioritization of these are the ones that we think are hot. And I think that's great, but it leaves you with a very short list. And that's the problem."*

> *"if the assumption is everybody that could ever launch a... card program or is considering launching a car[d] program has already... has a processor, that's obviously not correct."*

The finding is no longer Novosapien's analysis of the client's framework. It is the framework's author saying his own document does not do what he wants.

## The targeting model Ian actually wants

Stated in full at 00:59, and it is a clean funnel:

| Step | Filter | Effect |
|------|--------|--------|
| 1 | **Region.** TXN can only operate in Europe | *"you can discount all of those bunch gone"* |
| 2 | **Verticals with known card use cases.** Which industries have card program use cases that exist today | *"if you're... petrochemical or or whatever it might be out"* |
| 3 | **The three states.** Where the company sits in the cycle | This is where the ICP applies |

Company size does not rule people out at any step: *"if you're a startup thinking of launching the card program then we we work with you."*

**Everything that survives step 2 is a target.** The distinction that matters is not who is in and who is out, it is who you go to first.

> *"There are verticals and use cases that we know um the platform is built for and we know are prevalent to somewhat to a certain or lesser degree in those verticals. Everybody in those verticals is a target. It's the prioritization of those targets and the scheduleuling [scheduling] of them that's the most important thing."*

### Verticals do not vary by ICP

Ian said this three separate times and it did not land in the working session, which is part of why the session lost the thread. The three ICP states are **states**, not company types, so the vertical list underneath them is identical:

> *"So the so proposed verticals will be the same irrespective of whether or not it's one, two or three on the ICP because typically um yeah again I don't see that I don't see the answer would be any different in any of them."*

> *"What separates your three is not who the company is, it's what the what state it's in."*

### The ranking correction

Ian's one change to the matrix the model produced:

> *"in a target vertical no evidence of a program no evidence in market is higher than has a program contract position open or or unknown."*

A greenfield company in a target vertical outranks a company that already has a program whose contract position is unknown. His reason: the second one is not greenfield, and *"there's not going to be any immediate movement."*

That ranking is the exact inverse of what the v0.7 register produces today, where migration accounts average 74.4 points against 61.7 for greenfield and take 17 of the 22 Priority 1 places ([[qualification-matrix]]).

### In-market and has-a-program are two axes, not one scale

> *"has a program but is in market is basically a suggestion of a change to the incumbent which again you're not selling why launch a car[d] program you're selling why why use us whereas... doesn't have a card program but is in market um is a slightly different process and sales process."*

The two populations arrive carrying different things:

- **Has an incumbent and is in market.** Comes in *"with a bunch of knowns and experiences they've had that they're going to want to make sure they're not going to have with you again. Otherwise, they wouldn't be moving."*
- **No program and in market.** Has *"likely made up their mind that they should launch a car[d] program"* but brings *"none of the baggage of a bad previous experience with one of our competitors."*

> *"A company with an incumbent could actively be in market as well as a company with no program is in market and they're trying to find their first processor."*

## The two-stage sale

The single most useful piece of messaging structure to come out of the session, and it follows directly from the greenfield ruling.

Where the card is a **value-add** rather than the core product, you cannot assume the prospect has decided to launch at all. So the sale has two stages, in order:

1. **Why launch a card program in the first place.** Ian: *"act as the person almost put yourself in the shoes of being someone as the internal champion as why somebody should launch that program. You're the person that's bringing the idea to them."*
2. **Why do it with TXN.** *"If they think it's a good idea, then your second part is to sell why they should do it with you."*

> *"It's a very common sales process."*

And the warning that makes stage one non-optional even where a signal exists:

> *"Even if you've got a signal that you know that they're in the market, you shouldn't assume that they've made a decision that they're definitely going to launch a car[d] program. So to demonstrate expertise and knowledge in their industry, you should talk about why they should launch a program."*

Ian has already done this himself in Claude for the investment trading platform vertical, *"exactly on those lines."*

### Which means step two is vertical and use case pairs

Ian's own conclusion, once he had followed the logic through:

> *"If that's right, then step two isn't the list of 12 verticals. It's a list of vertical and use case pairs."*

Because whether the card is core or value-add is a property of the **vertical plus the use case**, not of the company, and it decides whether stage one of the sale is needed. Brett's read back:

> *"it's quite interesting how it's flipping the ICP to going it's people where card is a core product across the different verticals and card is a value ad which is actually how you want to talk to them"*

### The artefact Ian asked for

> *"it needs almost I feel like it just needs a simple matrix that can be followed so that everybody understands exactly what the difference is between a legitimate target... priority target right now. And somebody that should be disqualified."*

Three states, one page, no ambiguity. This is a named deliverable now.

## Exclusions, clarified

### Tier 1 banks: the reason is bureaucracy, not size

The anti-profile excludes tier 1 banks, governments and local governments. Ian gave the reason, and it is not prestige or deal size:

> *"the reason that that tier one banks are an exclusion is my experience is small companies are just not ready to do the the heavy lifting... you can get killed and they'll keep you along and you can be a part of the ride and they'll say they love your technology and then you get landed with the most ridiculous amount of documentation and and bureaucracy that small companies are not built for and we're not. So the reason that banks, governments, local governments are excluded is because of that."*

**A tier 1 bank's wholly owned digital subsidiary therefore cannot be excluded in advance.** It depends entirely on how tightly bound it is to the parent's compliance machinery:

- If the bank has handed over money and set loose oversight, *"then fine."*
- If the parent imposes its own due diligence, compliance and process expectations on the subsidiary, *"I'd be much less interested in that."*

And the awkward part: *"I'm not sure we can exclude them but we need to figure out a way of knowing early how closely integrated into the into the bank's processes they were and I'm not sure you can do that without conversation."* So this is a qualification question for a human on a call, not a filter.

His closing line on the whole category is worth keeping, because it is a rule about internal behaviour rather than about prospects:

> *"your job really is to kill the excitement in your organization and focus on things you can actually win until you can win. That's the truth in my experience."*

### Geography: the MVP markets are not a boundary

> *"there's reference to the fact that the MVP markets are going to be um the Eastern European markets. That does not mean that we are limiting our efforts even in the early outreach to just Eastern Europe. The reason we picked Eastern Europe is because we think that might be the place we can have the right level of success. Um, but we're not we're not restricting ourselves to that by any stretch of imagination."*

This matches [[icp-definition]] §4.4, which already says phasing governs prioritisation only. It needed saying because the model was reading the phase list as a filter.

On a Polish subsidiary of a non-EEA parent: not black and white. It turns on **who you contract with**, because contracting with an entity outside the EEA changes which rules and regulations the contract must satisfy. Ian's ruling:

> *"I would probably recommend not excluding people um and including them because the only way you're going to find out is by getting into a conversation."*

## Channel: there is no marketplace, and BIN sponsors are out of scope

Brett asked whether anything exists like G-Cloud, a public place where companies post RFPs for card issuing. Ian:

> *"There's no equivalent. There's no equivalent in the industry."*

Instead there are two channels, the **scheme** and the **BIN sponsor**, and three places a company can start building its vendor ecosystem. The two things that must be in place are *"a licensed entity which is the bin sponsor and the technology platform which is the processor which is us."* Order does not matter, which is why TXN wants to be on as many BIN sponsors' vendor lists as possible and vice versa.

**The Marqeta precedent matters for positioning.** Because Marqeta was a well-known brand with the leading platform, fintech startups came to the processor **first**:

> *"the most important thing to them was we need to know that the platform we're going to work with can drive our product not just now but in the future. So, we're going to find the platform first, find out who they work with from a bin sponsor point of view."*

**BIN sponsor outreach is explicitly outside the Outbound Workforce.** Ian and Dorte run it directly: *"nothing to do on an outreach perspective um through this process. But yes, Dorte and I will be establishing those relationships."* The list is not big enough to warrant automation, TXN is getting the first one on board with a view to publicising it, and the population is shrinking, both from regulatory tightening in Europe and from acquisitions by processors. Ian is sceptical of any acquired sponsor still claiming independence.

## Process and delivery

### Personas move offline

Brett is taking the persona build as a heavy lift rather than a workshop. Roughly **half an hour of processing per persona**, run over the following 24 hours, delivered as a single HTML artifact for asynchronous review.

The reasoning is worth recording because it sets the expected quality bar: *"it doesn't need to be word for word 100% perfect... these artifacts are designed for the agents to pick up and work"*, with the agent building its own knowledge base as it works, using the persona as a baseline against per-target research and adjusting from what converts.

Brett will ping for anything he genuinely cannot resolve rather than hold everyone in a session.

> *"you set up the framework and put all of the all the documents in and then for some reason it forgets to read what it's already said it's going to do um every time."*
