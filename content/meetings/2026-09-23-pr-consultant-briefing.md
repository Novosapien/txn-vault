## The positioning collision

The most consequential exchange in the session, and it needs stating carefully because nobody in it is behaving badly.

### What was agreed

Bron asked whether the Control Center could be described as up and running. Brett said no, still waiting on APIs. Nicola's response:

> *"From an external comms perspective, we should be pushing to just announce it as being, like, that is what we are launching. I think it's a technicality that it's not actually live, because **no prospect in their right mind is going to be ready to onboard in the next three weeks anyway.** So by the time they've caught up with the process... they can understand what the proposition is in that sales or pre-sales phase."*

> *"In terms of the terminology that we're talking about for external comms, we should really be quite bullish about the full proposition and not get ourselves tied up in knots in terms of what exactly is available when."*

Bron agreed, and named the obstacle:

> *"Agreed. **We need to just get Ian on board with that.** He doesn't like to say things that are not available, but I think we can explain it... **we can get him comfortable with that.**"*

> *"It'll be there, but we're not going to highlight it."*

**Both were explicit that this is not about misleading anyone.** Nicola: *"We don't need to. I don't like to lie either. We can make sure it's [accurate]."* Their argument is a fair one: nothing in the sales cycle moves fast enough for a three-week gap to matter.

### Why it still needs recording

**It runs against a rule Ian set himself, thirteen days earlier**, and it is being settled in a room he was not in.

**Novosapien is not a bystander here.** [[offer]] carries the rule as written policy, set from Ian's own words on 24 August: *"AI is the mechanism that delivers outcomes; it is never TXN's identity claim, and messages never lead with the technology."* The Cold Outreach Workforce will ground on whichever document wins, so this is a configuration input, not only a client marketing decision.

**And Brett's own reply to the demo question sits on the other side of it.** Asked about scale and access, he described the demo environment as *"a fully functioning tool that you can essentially call it a sandbox"*, which is accurate, and the difference between that and *"this is what we are launching"* is precisely the distinction Ian drew.

Raised at [[open-questions]] #96.

### The "truly agentic" claim

Separately, and it will be heard as the same thing.

> Brett: *"**TXN is a truly agentic AI business.** The management team use agentic AI. The code's built agentically, that built an agentic platform. I've never seen a business truly agentic like that."*

Bron liked it immediately and wants it promoted:

> *"That's actually not one of our messaging pillars... I really quite like what Brett is saying. It's a truly agentic business, has been built agentically, the tool's been built agentically."*

Nicola's reason for wanting it is the strongest commercial argument on the call: *"to the conversation that we've already had around how commoditised a lot of this industry is, to have something that we can start to pull on as the point of difference, I think would be quite valuable."* Bron: *"it's a nice lever."*

**The two statements are not the same claim and should not be merged.** *Built using agentic tools* is a statement about how TXN works, and it is true: Michael is AI-first, the Console was prototyped agentically, the GTM corpus was built this way. *An agentic AI business* is a statement about what the product is, and Ian has already rejected its neighbour: *"we're not an AI native company. Let's be really clear about this. The platform hasn't been built thinking about AI first."*

Bron also noted, in the same conversation: **"Ian doesn't want to say AI powered at all."** And on the existing pillar: *"we do have it, but we don't actually use the word agentic."*

So there are now three positions in play, and only one is written down.

## The content platform, as demonstrated

Recorded because Nicola and Bron will work in it, and because several mechanics were stated for the first time.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Entities** | Multiple per account. TXN has the company plus Ian, Michael and Dorte, **linked** so individuals can post in their own brand or for TXN |
| **Users** | Nicola and Bron can both be given access; the pipeline is visible to everyone |
| **Per platform** | Format rules applied per channel. *"You can't just take a LinkedIn article and slap it into Substack"* |
| **Formats** | Text, infographics, carousels, all on loaded brand kit. **Video deliberately excluded**: *"if you're going to do video properly, you need to do video copy. Don't AI slop it"* |
| **Content types** | Lead magnet, explainer, thought leadership, each shaping structure |
| **Pillars** | Required, with a **no-pillar option** that infers the right one, because users forget what their pillars meant |
| **Audiences** | Optional, and they shape tone. Brett's TXN example: *"a CEO of a fintech that could possibly start a card division"* |
| **Campaigns** | For bulk themed runs, *"200 pieces of content on this theme, run it for a month"* |
| **Ideas** | Research topics **plus tracked influencers** *"that drop content first"*, scored on trend relevance, brand alignment and predicted performance |
| **Nova** | The conversational agent, reachable **on WhatsApp**, for ideas captured on the move |
| **External posts** | Content posted elsewhere can be pasted in for a consolidated view |
| **Scale** | *"We're running about 700 to 750 agents all in parallel"* per generation |

**The deliberate design choice, stated twice:** the tool does not advise. Brett: *"we tried to do that in the very beginning, guide the whole time, be the expert in the room. What we found is you go in a circle... whereas what we found was we take control and we go, guess what, this is what you're getting. Now review it."*

**Why Tyler exists in the product**, and it is honest: *"everyone gets excited in the first two weeks, everyone posts four, five, six pieces of content, and then it goes quiet because people run out of ideas."* An ideas feature helped and did not solve it. Tyler's fortnightly session did. *"At the end of the day, it's the idea that's the hard part."*

**What is being built:** engagement and comment tracking, feedback loops, then content shaped by what the audience responds to rather than what the brand wants to say, then recommendation engines. Brett on the constraint: *"it's a big lift... already we're at about 85, 86 accounts and we have terabytes of data."*

**Bron's feature request:** competitor post tracking. Not built, but Brett said the ideas and influencer monitoring is the same mechanism pointed at a different list, *"you just have your competitor list."*
