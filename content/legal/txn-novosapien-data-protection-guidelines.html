Data Protection Guidelines
TXN Knowledge Hub AI Assistant: interim hosting by Novosapien

Version 1.0 Date 13 November 2026 Parties TXN Global Limited and Novosapien Global Ltd Status Working document

This is the agreed factual position on personal data in the TXN Knowledge Hub AI assistant while Novosapien hosts it. It is the companion to the Data Processing Agreement (Interim Hosting): this document explains what happens to data and why, the Agreement makes it binding. It is written to be given to TXN's counsel, to answer Direct Transact's interim launch questions in one place, and to be updated as the position changes.

The one thing to read first. For this interim period Novosapien hosts the AI assistant, in Novosapien's own Azure subscription, using Novosapien's own model account. That makes Microsoft, Google and Sentry Novosapien's sub-processors, and makes Novosapien accountable for the hosting itself. That is a different position from the rest of the engagement, where Direct Transact hosts and those providers are TXN's. It is the reason a separate agreement is needed now rather than later, and it reverses at Production Cutover.

1What the interim service is, and what it is not

The interim service is the documentation co-pilot and the documentation server behind the Knowledge Hub. It answers a developer's question from TXN's published documentation. It runs in Novosapien's Azure subscription in North Europe (Ireland) until it moves to hosting provided by Direct Transact in the TXN Azure subscription.

It doesIt does not
  • Answer questions from TXN's public documentation
  • Run example API calls against a mock API
  • Let a visitor return to their own chat within 72 hours
  • Rate limit per visitor to protect the service
  • Hold any TXN client data
  • Hold any cardholder data
  • Hold any TXN credentials
  • Reach TxnGlobal.API
  • Require a sign-in, or hold names or email addresses
  • Carry over the pilot sign-up records

That scope is written into clause 1.2 of the Agreement and cannot change without TXN's written agreement. It is also what keeps the risk small: the most sensitive thing the service can hold is text a visitor chose to type.

The IP address never reaches the model. The only reason it exists at all is Azure Front Door, which needs it to run a firewall and a per-visitor rate limit on a surface with no sign-in. No model provider sees it, and neither does the browser identifier.

Where 72 hours came from

It is a functional number, not a regulatory one. Comparable services commonly use 24 hours. TXN chose 72 so a visitor can return to a chat the next day, while keeping the exposure short. It is the same number TXN has asked Direct Transact to apply to the public sandbox, which is still to be confirmed with Direct Transact in writing.

4The lawful basis

TXN owns the lawful basis as controller. The position the parties have taken, and the reasoning behind it:

ProcessingBasisWhy
Firewall and security logs (IP addresses) Legitimate interest, Article 6(1)(f) Recital 49 names network and information security as a legitimate interest, and gives prevention of unauthorised access and denial-of-service attacks as examples. Thirty days is unremarkable for this purpose
Chat content and the browser identifier Legitimate interest, Article 6(1)(f) The visitor has asked for the service. The identifier exists only so they get their own chat back
Sanitised chats, to improve the documentation Legitimate interest, Article 6(1)(f) The browser identifier is stripped, so the retained record cannot be tied back to a visitor. The purpose is improving TXN's own documentation

Open item. A short written legitimate interest assessment does not exist yet. EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 set a three-step test: a lawful interest, necessity, and a balance against the individual's rights. One page covers it for each of the three rows above. It should be in place before the privacy notice is finalised.

Is an IP address personal data? Yes, and it is treated as such.

An IP address is personal data where the holder has a lawful route to link it to a person, which the Court of Justice established in Breyer (C-582/14); Recital 30 names IP addresses as online identifiers. The parties do not argue the point. The firewall logs are treated as holding personal data, with a basis, a retention period and a line in the privacy notice.

And the random browser identifier?

Also personal data while it exists. Article 4(1) lists an online identifier as a means of identification, and EDPS v SRB (C-413/23 P, September 2025) confirmed the test is whether the holder has means reasonably likely to identify the person. The exposure is small and getting smaller: it survives one tab session in the browser, the server deletes it 72 hours after the last activity, it is stripped from anything kept longer, and the model never receives it.

5The principles both parties operate to

These are the working rules behind the Agreement. They came out of the alignment call on 11 November 2026, and they are what should be applied to any question this document does not answer.

PrincipleWhat it means
Personal data is never asked for No field, prompt or journey requests it. The acceptable use policy tells visitors not to enter it. If a visitor enters it anyway, nothing is done with it beyond the retention periods above. The sandbox reinforces this: the APIs need no cardholder name or address, so a completely blank cardholder can be created
Store it in Europe, and keep access inside Europe or adequacy Everything is stored in Europe. Model inference runs on the EU multi-region endpoint, never a global one. The only access from outside the EU is Novosapien's engineers in the United Kingdom, which the European Commission's adequacy decision covers
The control is the agreement, not a claim that access is impossible Novosapien's engineers can technically open the Front Door logs. Nobody can credibly promise otherwise in any IT system. What matters is what access is granted, what anyone would use it for, and what happens if it is misused. Clause 6.2 of the Agreement makes the limit binding, requires every access to be by a named person and logged, and forbids approaching an internet provider to identify an IP address
Minimise what links one record to another The chain of browser to identifier to chat to IP address is broken at the one point it could have been joined: the identifier moves to session storage, and is stripped from anything kept beyond 72 hours
Risk-based, proportionate Controls are sized to a public documentation assistant that holds no client data, not to a system handling cardholder records. Where a control costs more than the risk it removes, say so and record the decision rather than engineering around a hypothetical
Write the position down once, and keep it current This document and the Agreement are the single answer to a questionnaire, a privacy notice, a counsel review or a Direct Transact control. When the service changes, they change in the same week

6Browser storage, and whether a consent banner is needed

Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive requires consent to store information on a visitor's device, but exempts storage strictly necessary for a service the visitor has asked for. EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 confirm the rule covers browser storage and not only cookies, and WP29 Opinion 04/2012 treats a session identifier for a user-requested function as exempt.

The browser identifier exists only to give the visitor their own chat back, and it lasts one tab session. The exemption applies, so no consent banner is needed for it. Cyprus applies the rule through its own national law, and TXN's counsel should confirm the local implementation.

7What the privacy notice has to say

The notice is TXN's, and it needs four things for this service:

  • The purposes and the lawful basis for each of the three rows in section 4.
  • The retention periods: 30 days for security logs, 72 hours after the last message for chats.
  • The recipients, or the categories of recipient (Article 13(1)(e)).
  • A line telling visitors not to enter personal data, matching the acceptable use policy.

Does it have to name Microsoft and Google?

No, not in the notice itself. The WP29 transparency guidelines (WP260) say to name recipients where possible and otherwise give categories that are as specific as possible, so "cloud hosting provider" and "AI model provider" is sufficient. But in Österreichische Post (C-154/21) the Court held that a person making an access request under Article 15 is entitled to the actual recipients. So TXN must be able to name them on request, which is what Annex C of the Agreement provides. The practical observation from the 11 November call holds: naming a cloud provider in a privacy notice is unusual, and TXN's sample notices are worth checking before deciding.

8Access, deletion and other data subject requests

The service has no accounts and holds no names or email addresses. Article 11 says a controller does not have to collect extra data purely to identify a person, and Articles 15 to 20 then do not apply unless the person supplies information that allows identification.

RequestWhat happens
"Delete my data", with no identifierTXN cannot find the chats, and is not required to collect more data in order to. The chats delete themselves 72 hours after the last message in any case
"Delete my data", with a chat identifierNovosapien deletes or exports that chat on TXN's instruction within 5 Business Days, provided it has not already expired
A request about the pilot sign-up recordsOut of scope here. Those records are not on the interim service and do not migrate to it
A request received by Novosapien directlyPassed to TXN without responding. TXN answers as controller
