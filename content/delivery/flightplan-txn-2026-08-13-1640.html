The six weeks at a glance

The thing to keep saying out loud

We stand features up in days, and making them dependable takes considerably longer. That refinement is what these timelines protect. The agent stack went from empty repository to deployed and demonstrable in roughly five working days; the remaining weeks are not more of the same, they are edge cases, permissions, and the dependencies we do not control.

How the effort actually breaks down

Two separate pieces of work, and they are routinely confused. Sprint Zero is the requirements effort for the entire project: the backlog, the architecture, the dependency map, the component deep-dives. It is delivered and invoiced at £17,500 and it produced the vault. It has nothing to do with the console.

The Control Center React build is separate. It is a full React build of the GUI environments, done as additional work for the agentic experiment so the agent had real surfaces to drive rather than a mock-up. It ran in the week before kickoff and appears in no priced stage of any proposal. That is the gap worth naming: it is real delivered engineering carrying no line of its own.

Explicitly not in this build

The cheapest disappointment prevention available. None of the following ships on 7 September, and TXN should plan on their absence.

  • Permission scoping. The MCP server is a deliberate all-access monolith. Per-user tool gating was consciously deferred (vault, 28 July and 3 August). Nothing in the pilot enforces who may do what.
  • A two-person approval queue. In-chat approval is the only gate. The maker-checker queue is agreed as a follow-on.
  • Any connection to the real Direct Transact API. The pilot runs entirely on the mock. That was the design, and it is why the pilot did not stall.
  • Real personal data. Synthetic only, per the Pilot Order.
  • Alert detection. The agent can read and triage alerts; nothing owns raising them.
  • Cardholder onboarding as an agent workflow. Not yet implemented. See the workflow status page below.
  • Sub-agents. Scoped in the architecture, not wired.

Workflow status

The Novosapien candidate slate, Ten new workflows, you choose what earns a build, set against the implemented code. This page states what is operational against each workflow, and what each remaining candidate costs to build.

Alignment required before the slate is reissued. The slate describes three workflows as operational in the pilot. The implemented set is six SOPs, and it differs from the three described. Spend-control change matches its description. Lost or stolen card is partially implemented: the suspend and reactivate chain is operational, the block, replace and re-provision chain is not. Recommendation

Candidate 8 is the recommended next build. It is the flagship of the original vision, it requires no new tools, and the agent already runs both halves separately (review-alerts reads, the spend-control SOP acts). It is the shortest route to a complete demonstration for TXN's board.

Candidate 2 appears simple but has no tool coverage: none of its six endpoints exist today. It should not be sequenced ahead of candidates 1, 3 and 8 on the basis of apparent simplicity.

The two questions the slate asks TXN to settle

  1. Who is the pilot's operator? Customer-service agent or programme administrator. Candidates 2 and 3 land differently depending on the answer, and so does the field-level detail work already flagged by Michael.
  2. The data lake is the common blocker. Candidate 9 and two bench items depend on analytics data Direct Transact has not delivered. Demonstrate on staged data now, or hold until the data exists. Our recommendation is to hold: a weak version damages the idea more than waiting does.
