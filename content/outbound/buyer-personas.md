## Selection: why eleven, and why not a tidy twelve

Personas were chosen by who actually buys in each ICP, taken from that ICP's own buying group rather than from a template. The guideline allows one to five per ICP and targets two to three; the count here follows the ICPs and stops where they stop.

| ICP | Personas built | Why that number |
|-----|----------------|-----------------|
| **ICP 1** | CPO, CTO, economic buyer | Three roles named, and each meets exposure from a different direction. The CPO has promised something publicly, the CTO must approve something nobody in the building understands, and the person holding the money is funding a line with no comparable in the plan. None can answer the others' question |
| **ICP 2** | CPO, CTO, CFO | Three roles named. The CTO is built in full but their **weighting is an open question**, because ICP 2 is the only situation where a client runs two processors in parallel, and that either makes the CTO central or barely involves them |
| **ICP 3** | CPO, CFO | Two, and two is complete. The ICP explicitly places the CTO outside the buying group at this stage: they arrive after the decision to have a card programme has been taken, which is after the sale this ICP runs. A technical persona here would be padding aimed at somebody not in the room |
| **ICP 4** | CTO, CPO, CFO | Three roles named, and unusually the CTO leads, because the deciding question is whether moving is survivable. That leadership is **proposed and not yet confirmed by Ian** |

**No role is reused across ICPs.** There are three CPO personas, three CTO personas and four economic buyers, and each is a different human because the dominant pain differs. The ICP 1 CTO has never issued a card; the ICP 2 CTO is about to run two estates; the ICP 4 CTO is planning an operation on a live cardholder base. Distinctness is the load-bearing property here and it is checked at the gate below.

## Two personas cannot be messaged yet, and one ICP cannot be operated

Recorded here so nobody discovers it at campaign build time.

**ICP 3, both personas, blocked on the peer map.** ICP 3 qualifies a company by inference from a named peer in the same vertical who already runs a card programme. That peer is both the qualification evidence and the opening line of the message. **The maintained peer list does not exist**, and it is the largest outstanding piece of work on this ICP. Until it is built, neither ICP 3 persona is usable.

**ICP 4, all three personas, gated twice.** Reference customers, because nobody moves a full card suite to a company with no track record. And migration tooling, because Ian's position on 3 September is that buyers now expect it: *"they don't expect a 15 year old approach"*. The personas exist so the work is done when both gates clear, not started then.

## The rules that bind every message to every persona

Carried from [[offer]] and from [[icp-definition]] Section 8.1.12, and repeated here because the persona documents are what message agents read most often.

| Rule | Scope |
|------|-------|
| **Never name a competitor of TXN's** | Every persona, every channel. Contrast against categories only, even if the prospect names one first |
| **Never criticise the incumbent**, named, implied or by category | Hardest in ICP 2, where the incumbent stays and the buyer is standing behind a live decision. Applies in ICP 4 too, where the buyer has already decided the incumbent failed them: they may say it, TXN does not |
| **Never lead with the owners** | Direct Transact and Paycorp do not appear in first touch or the follow-up sequence. They are credibility placed later, deployed by a person |
| **No pricing figures, ever** | Ground on the shape. Figures are a conversation for a human |
| **Never describe how the platform was built** | The claim is that the platform is TXN's. No code lineage, in either direction |
| **Never describe the launch process** | The under-ten-day figure is quotable as a stated target. The mechanism behind it is not written down, so it may not be described. See [[offer]] Section 2 step 2 |
| **Never offer to share the reissue cost** | ICP 4 only. Ian described it as something TXN could consider. It is not an existing lever |
| **Naming a prospect's peer is not the same as naming a competitor** | ICP 3 only, where the peer is the qualification evidence and the opening. What may never be said is which processor that peer used |

## The gate, run mechanically on 4 September 2026

Every check from the guideline's depth contract and the persona track's Phase 3.

| # | Check | Result |
|---|-------|--------|
| 1 | **All 12 sections present on every persona** | **Pass.** Verified programmatically across all eleven. No section missing on any |
| 2 | **Section minimums** | **Pass.** Core responsibilities 5 against a minimum of 4 to 5. Psychographic dimensions 4 against 4. Operational pains 3 and strategic pains 3, each against 3. Prior attempts 4 against 3. Criteria 4 and objections 4, each against 3. Triggers 5 against 3. Information diet 5 against 3. Success metrics 4 against 3. Titles 6 against 4 to 6 |
| 3 | **Day in the life is a narrative of at least five or six sentences that makes the friction felt** | **Pass.** All eleven are first-person, six to nine sentences, and none is a summary of the role |
| 4 | **No recycled pains, day-in-the-life or messages across personas** | **Pass.** Checked pair-wise within each ICP and across the three CPO, three CTO and four economic-buyer personas. The recycling risk was highest across the CTOs and each carries a different problem: validating an unknown domain, absorbing a second estate, and surviving a live migration |
| 5 | **Section 11 promises only what the offer claims** | **Pass.** Every message section was written against [[offer]] Sections 2 and 4. Three were tightened during authoring: the ten-day target is stated as a target and never as a process, compliance is stated as confidence rather than replacement, and the reissue figure is attributed as Ian's industry estimate rather than a TXN price |
| 6 | **Every persona's deepest fear connects to its ICP's dominant pain** | **Pass.** Exposure, constraint, falling behind and entrapment each land in all of their ICP's personas, and no persona's fear would sit better in a different ICP |
| 7 | **First-person voice throughout Sections 4 and 5** | **Pass.** No third-person distance anywhere. Quotes are written as speech, not as paraphrase |
| 8 | **Density at or above the gold standard, and no filler to hit a minimum** | **Pass on density.** See the honest qualification below |
| 9 | **Vocabulary check** | **Pass.** No banned word, no section symbol, no em dash. "Programme" appears only in reference to a card programme in British spelling contexts inherited from the ICP document, and "program" is used where TXN's own product spelling applies |
| 10 | **Buying group matches the ICP's, not the offer's** | **Pass.** Re-derived per ICP as the ICP instructs, which is why ICP 3 has two personas and ICP 4 leads with the CTO |

**The one honest qualification, and it is the important one.** Check 8 passes on density and cannot pass on provenance. The guideline's method starts from real deals: who signed, who resisted, what they said to a peer in their own words. **TXN is pre-launch and has no closed deals**, so there is no deal history to mine and no transcript to quote. Every first-person quote in these eleven personas is written from the ICP's dominant pain and its signal-to-pain mapping, not transcribed from a real buyer. That is the correct method available given the evidential state, and it is not the same thing as a grounded persona. The fastest way to fix it is not another authoring pass; it is Ian and Dorte reading the quotes and saying which ones a real buyer would never say.

## The gaps list

| # | What is missing | Who | Why it matters |
|---|-----------------|-----|----------------|
| P1 | **Real buyer language.** Every quote is derived rather than heard. | Ian and Dorte, by redlining | The quotes are the heart of the artefact and the thing message agents match tone against. Correcting voice is worth more than adding sections |
| P2 | **The ICP 3 peer map**: named companies per vertical who have launched a relevant card programme | Novosapien to build, Ian to sanity-check | Both ICP 3 personas are unusable without it, and inventing a peer is fabrication |
| P3 | **ICP 2 CTO weighting.** Central to the deal, or barely involved | Ian, from experience | It decides whether TXN raises the cost of running two processors or answers it only when asked |
| P4 | **ICP 4 buying group confirmation.** CTO leading is proposed, not ruled | Ian | It sets who the first message goes to in the ICP with the least observable evidence |
| P5 | **ICP 1 economic buyer: founder or CFO in practice** | Ian | ICP 1 has no employee band and sub-10 is in scope. The persona is written to cover both, which is a hedge rather than an answer |
| P6 | **Information diet, all eleven.** Derived from role and sector convention, not from anything TXN has observed | Ian, Dorte, and later the campaign data | This is the operationally expensive gap: it decides channel, timing and which publication is worth citing. It is also the section most likely to be quietly wrong |
| P7 | **Trigger events, all eleven.** Derived from the ICPs' observable signals rather than from what preceded real deals | Ian, then live data | Triggers drive timing. Derived triggers are plausible and untested |
| P8 | **Whether any persona should be cut.** Eleven is at the upper end of what four ICPs support | Ian | The guideline's instruction is never to pad. If a role does not genuinely buy, deleting it improves grading |

## Relationship to the three client-authored scaffolds

[[persona-champion]], [[persona-primary-user]] and [[persona-economic-buyer]] are Ian's v0.1 scaffolds, written between April and July 2026. They are **not superseded as source material** and their objection sections in particular fed the work above.

They are superseded **as the workforce artefact**, for two reasons. Each is written assuming an incumbent exists, which makes none of them usable for ICP 1 or ICP 3, and ICP 1 is the highest-priority ICP. And each describes one role across the whole market, whereas a persona in this model is a child of exactly one ICP and inherits that ICP's dominant pain, which is what makes grading discriminate.
