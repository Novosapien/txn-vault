## 1. The Core Tension

TXN is jointly and equally owned by Direct Transact and Paycorp — two established payments businesses that created TXN as a 50/50 joint venture. That ownership is a genuine credibility asset. The challenge is how to use it without undermining TXN's positioning as a modern, purpose-built platform.

Direct Transact has operated payment infrastructure for the majority of South Africa's clearing and settlement banks since 2002, processing over R50 billion monthly. That track record is part of TXN's foundation — not because TXN is built on top of DT's platform, but because DT is a co-founding owner whose 25-year track record is part of what TXN was created to deliver.

Two framing failures are common in TXN's current materials, each creating a different perception problem:

- The legacy signal: language like 'built on', 'core SA platform', and 'inherit infrastructure' makes TXN sound like a repackaged version of an existing product. Targets: European fintechs who want a modern platform, not a legacy system with a fresh interface.
- The outsourcing signal: language like 'partner Direct Transact' or 'processing partner' implies TXN contracted out its processing to a third party. This raises a different concern: TXN has no real infrastructure of its own and depends on a supplier who could be replaced or withdrawn. Neither is true — DT is an owner, not a vendor.
- The overclaim: 'bank-grade' is explicitly banned as it is ambiguous and difficult to meaningfully define. Use specific, attributable metrics instead.

The correct frame: DT's 25 years of processing reliability is TXN's processing reliability — not because TXN was built on DT's system, but because DT is a co-founding owner who brought that track record into the venture. The language must reflect ownership, not vendor dependency.

Direct Transact is not a service provider. It is not a partner in the commercial sense. It is one of two companies that created TXN. Its heritage is cited as ownership-derived credibility, not as contracted service provision.

## 4. How to Describe Each Owner

### 4.1 Direct Transact

Direct Transact is TXN's co-founding 50% owner. This is the most important framing correction in this document.

Describing Direct Transact as a 'partner' implies TXN contracted out its processing to a third party — raising an immediate concern: if TXN has outsourced its core infrastructure, what does TXN actually own? That framing undermines confidence rather than building it. Equally, describing TXN as 'built on' or 'powered by' DT's platform implies TXN is a skin on a legacy product.

The correct frame is ownership-anchored credibility. Direct Transact is one of two companies that created TXN. Its 25-year track record is not borrowed from a supplier — it is the processing heritage that one of TXN's founding owners brought into the venture. TXN was built to combine that proven reliability with a modern developer experience that DT's existing products do not offer.

The approved formulation: 'TXN combines an intelligent developer and operator experience with 25 years of proven processing heritage from Direct Transact, our co-founding owner' — or in short form: 'Direct Transact, our co-founding owner, with 25 years of processing heritage.' Do not use: built on, powered by, provided by, runs on, inherit, partner (vendor sense).

|---|---|
| ✔ TXN combines an intelligent developer and operator experience with 25 years of proven processing heritage from Direct Transact, our co-founding owner. | ✘ TXN runs on / is built on / is powered by Direct Transact's platform. |
| ✔ Direct Transact is one of TXN's two founding owners. It has operated at the heart of South Africa's banking system for 25 years — processing over R50 billion monthly. TXN was built to harness that proven foundation in a next-generation platform. | ✘ TXN's core processing infrastructure is provided by Direct Transact. ['Provided by' is vendor language — DT is an owner, not a supplier.] |
| ✔ Direct Transact, our co-founding owner, has processed over R50 billion monthly at 99.99% uptime for more than two decades. TXN's processing reliability is rooted in that track record. | ✘ When you build on TXN, you inherit Direct Transact's infrastructure. ['Inherit' implies dependency on an external system.] |
| ✔ Direct Transact holds Visa and Mastercard third-party processor certification and PASA System Operator status. As a co-founding owner of TXN, those certifications are part of TXN's foundation. | ✘ TXN is built on / is an evolution of / is a next-generation version of Direct Transact's platform. [Frames TXN as DT's product, not an independent platform.] |
| ✔ TXN was co-founded by the team behind Direct Transact — 25 years of operating payment infrastructure for SA's banks. They built TXN because they knew what was missing from legacy and earlier-generation platforms. | ✘ Direct Transact is our processing partner. ['Partner' reads as outsourced vendor, not co-founder and owner.] |

### 4.2 Paycorp

Paycorp is TXN's co-founding 50% owner — equal in ownership to Direct Transact. Founded in 1999, Paycorp operates across Southern Africa, Central and Eastern Europe, and the UK. It processes over $9 billion annually and has long-standing relationships with banks, card associations, payment bodies, and regulators across multiple markets.

The term 'parent group' has been retired from TXN's external messaging. It implied a subsidiary relationship — as if TXN sits beneath Paycorp in a corporate hierarchy. That is not accurate. TXN is a 50/50 joint venture: Paycorp and Direct Transact are equal co-founding owners, and TXN is the independently operating entity they created together. In external copy, Paycorp is always 'Paycorp, our co-founding owner' — the same designation used for Direct Transact.

Paycorp's contribution is international market reach, regulatory relationships, and commercial backing. It does not supply TXN's technology. The 25 years of expertise belongs to Paycorp as a company — cite it separately from Direct Transact's processing heritage.

When referencing both in one statement, keep the contributions distinct. Direct Transact brings processing heritage; Paycorp brings market reach and commercial backing. Both are co-founding owners with equal 50% stakes. Do not conflate their heritage claims as '50 years combined' — they are separate and must be attributed separately.

In a sales or due diligence conversation, if a prospect asks who owns TXN, give a direct and complete answer.

When a prospect asks directly for the full structure, answer accurately and completely as above, then return to contribution framing.

## 9. Internal Guidance — Summary Rules

Share with anyone who writes about TXN, presents TXN externally, or produces sales or marketing material.

### 9.1 The four tests

Before publishing or sharing any sentence referencing Direct Transact or Paycorp:

- Test 1 — Technology source: Does the sentence imply TXN's technology was built on or is dependent on DT's existing system? If yes, rewrite.
- Test 2 — Vendor/outsourcing signal: Does the sentence imply TXN contracted out its processing to a third-party supplier? If yes, rewrite. DT is an owner, not a vendor.
- Test 3 — Legacy signal: Could a European fintech or digital bank reader interpret this as 'TXN is an old SA banking platform'? If yes, rewrite.
- Test 4 — Return to TXN: Does the sentence end on the owner, or on what TXN delivers? Sentences about owners should always return focus to TXN.

### 9.2 What you can always say

- TXN is a next-generation issuer-processing platform.
- TXN combines an intelligent developer and operator experience with 25 years of proven processing heritage from Direct Transact, our co-founding owner.
- Direct Transact is one of TXN's two founding owners and holds a 50% stake in the business.
- Direct Transact has operated at the heart of South Africa's banking system for 25 years — R50bn+ monthly, 99.99% uptime, Visa and Mastercard certification, PCI DSS.
- TXN's processing reliability is rooted in Direct Transact's 25-year track record — via ownership, not as an outsourced service.
- Paycorp, our co-founding owner, brings 25 years of international payments expertise across Southern Africa, Central and Eastern Europe, and the UK.
- TXN was built because the founding teams of Direct Transact and Paycorp knew what was missing from every existing platform.

### 9.3 What you must never say

- "bank-grade" — banned per Core Messaging v2.5 as an overclaim. Use specific metrics instead.
- TXN runs on / is built on / is powered by Direct Transact's platform. [Legacy signal — frames TXN as DT's product]
- TXN is an enhancement, evolution, or next-generation version of Direct Transact. [Same problem]
- TXN inherits / customers inherit infrastructure from Direct Transact. [Both legacy and outsourcing signal]
- Direct Transact is TXN's partner / processing partner. [Outsourcing signal — DT is a 50% co-founding owner, not a vendor]
- TXN has outsourced / contracted its processing to Direct Transact. [Factually incorrect and commercially damaging]
- TXN's core SA platform [with any qualifier]. [Wrong market, wrong frame]
- "50 years combined experience" — the two heritage claims are separate and must be attributed separately.
- "23 years" — the canonical figure from Core Messaging v2.5 is 25 years. Standardise on 25.
- "EMEA" as TXN's market — the ICP is European (EEA + UK). SA is origin context for processing heritage, not the target market.
