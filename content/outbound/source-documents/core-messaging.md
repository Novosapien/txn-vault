## 3. ICP and audience segments

TXN serves a specific slice of the card issuer processing market: European fintechs, digital banks, and embedded finance companies with 20 to 2,000 employees that are either launching a new card program or migrating an existing program from an incumbent processor. The full ICP is defined in TXN_GTM_ICP_Definition_v0.3. This section summarises the audience segments Core Messaging addresses.

### 3.1 In-scope segments

Three segment clusters carry most of the in-scope addressable market. The primary segmentation axis is sub-sector, not company stage, because most ICP-fit companies sit clearly in one of these three clusters regardless of stage.

| Segment cluster | What matters to them |
|---|---|
| Digital Banking and Neobanks | Product differentiation, cardholder experience, speed of feature launch, unit economics. The highest-priority sub-sector under ICP v0.3 Section 4.2, with the strongest competitor reference base (22 published client references across the six named competitors). Companies at this segment compete hard on product, and the processor's capability either enables or constrains that competition. |
| Commercial Card and Expense Management | Cost base at scale, platform reliability, program consolidation, reconciliation efficiency. Product differentiation matters less here than operational fit and margin. Expense management platforms and corporate card programs live or die on unit economics and scheme fee discipline. |
| Lending, FX, and Embedded Finance | Flexibility across use cases, integration simplicity, real-time data, cross-border support, scheme and BIN sponsor flexibility. A card program is one surface of a broader product, so the processor must support varied unit economics and allow the customer to keep control of their proposition. |

All three segments operate in the EEA plus the United Kingdom. Outbound priority is driven by the phasing in ICP v0.3 Section 4.4: MVP markets (Poland, Czech Republic, Romania, Hungary), Phase 1a (Southern Europe), Phase 1b (Western Europe), and the rest of EEA plus UK as opportunistic.

### 3.2 Who this isn't for

Core Messaging is not written for the audiences in the ICP v0.3 Section 7 anti-profile. Marketing and sales effort against these audiences is explicitly discouraged:

- Tier 1 banks (for example Barclays, BNP Paribas, HSBC, Deutsche Bank).
- Governments and local governments, including state-owned enterprises.
- Sub-scale operators running very small card programs in small geographic markets.
- Petrol retail companies (bespoke fuel card dynamics sit outside TXN's value proposition).
- Companies looking for bespoke, non-platform-based solutions where the IP rests with the company.
- Companies looking for on-premises hosting.
- Companies with limited capital for investment and operational expenses.
- Companies in administration, insolvency, or pending wind-down.
- Companies under sanctions exposure or reported to be in regulatory difficulty.

Inbound enquiries from anti-profile audiences are politely declined or deferred and recorded against the account in the Qualification Matrix.

### 3.3 Persona and role references

Three persona archetypes drive decision-making inside the in-scope segments. Each has a dedicated profile document; all three sit within ICP_&_Personas in the library.

- Champion: CPO or Head of Product. DACI Contributor. Shapes requirements, pushes the deal forward internally. Has the clearest upside from a processor change. See TXN_GTM_Persona_Champion_v0.1.
- Economic Buyer: CFO as primary, CEO or Founder as secondary for strategic deals. DACI Approver with veto on commercial viability. Most deals fail at this gate. See TXN_GTM_Persona_Economic_Buyer_v0.1.
- Primary User: CTO or Head of Engineering. DACI Approver with technical veto. The engineering team is the primary integrator and operator of TXN's APIs. See TXN_GTM_Persona_Primary_User_v0.1.

Role-level positioning across all five deal-relevant roles (CTO, CPO, Head of Payments, CFO, Risk and Compliance) is held in TXN_Role_Based_Messaging_(V1). Head of Payments (Driver in DACI) and Risk and Compliance (Contributor) are not one of the three primary archetypes but are critical supporting roles in most deals.

## 10. Tone of voice

Tone is how messaging survives contact with the real world: agencies, contractors, AI tools, and new joiners. The table below is the minimum shared contract everyone writing in TXN's voice should hold to.

| Aspect | Guidance |
|---|---|
| We sound like | Confident, plain-spoken, technically fluent. We respect the reader's time and intelligence. We say what we do and what it costs the customer not to have us. |
| We don't sound like | Vendor jargon. Hype. Empty superlatives. Generic payments marketing speak. We avoid revolutionary, disrupt, seamless, cutting-edge, best-in-class as lazy fillers. |
| Words to use | Platform. Processing. Program. Console. API. Agentic. Reliable. Proven. Flexible. Issuer. Scheme. Cardholder. Digital banking. Embedded finance. Expense management. Lending. FX. |
| Words to avoid | Solution (when platform is meant). Ecosystem (when partners is meant). Bank-grade (overclaim). Enterprise (out of scope per ICP). Revolutionary. Game-changing. World-class. Cutting-edge. Synergies. Leverage (as a verb). Partner (when referring to Direct Transact in a vendor or service-provider sense): Direct Transact is a co-founding 50% owner of TXN, not a contracted processing partner. Use 'Direct Transact, our co-founding owner' instead. |
| British English | Default to British spellings: realise, colour, analyse, behaviour, organisation. Avoid em dashes; use commas, parentheses, colons, or full stops instead. Exception: use 'program' and 'programs' (not 'programme/programmes') throughout — TXN's platform uses this spelling and consistency with the product takes precedence over British convention. |
| Numbers and claims | Every quantitative claim must have an owner and a source. No rounding up. If we cannot cite it, we do not claim it. |
| Heritage claim precision | The 25 years of processing heritage belongs to Direct Transact, TXN's co-founding owner. Attribute it when using the claim. TXN as a company has a shorter operating history. Direct Transact is not a vendor; it is a 50% co-founding owner. The heritage is cited via the ownership relationship, not as a contracted service. |
| AI messaging anchor | The approved AI messaging anchor phrase is 'Intelligence built in. Complexity taken out.' Use it verbatim — do not paraphrase or vary the two halves independently. Both halves connect to specific client outcomes defined in TXN_AI_Target_Outcomes_Framework_v1.0. Asset-by-asset copy adaptations are in TXN_AI_Messaging_Framework_v1.0. AI is positioned as the mechanism that delivers outcomes; it is not TXN's identity claim. Never lead with technology — lead with what the client achieves. |
| JV ownership language | The canonical external designation for both co-founding owners is 'Direct Transact, our co-founding owner' and 'Paycorp, our co-founding owner'. Never use: built on, powered by, provided by, runs on, inherit, partner (vendor sense), parent group, underlying infrastructure, bank-grade, or '50 years combined experience'. When describing both owners, keep their heritage claims separate and attributed: Direct Transact brings processing heritage (25 years, R50bn+ monthly, scheme certifications); Paycorp brings international market reach and commercial backing ($9bn annually, operations across Southern Africa, Central and Eastern Europe, UK). Sentences referencing an owner must return focus to TXN — end on what TXN delivers, not on the owner. Full approved and banned formulations, the four messaging tests, and talking points for ownership questions are in TXN_JV_Messaging_Framework_v2.1.docx. |

## 14. Tier calibration guidance

The ICP v0.3 scoring framework in Section 8 assigns accounts to Priority 1, Priority 2, Priority 3, or Disqualified tiers. Core Messaging is calibrated to tier as follows. The Qualification Matrix carries the tier per account; this section describes how messaging is adapted to tier once the classification is known.

### 14.1 Tier-level messaging approach

| Tier | Score range | Messaging approach |
|---|---|---|
| Priority 1 | 80 to 100 | Full positioning. All five pillars deployed. Persona-indexed proof points for Champion, Primary User, and Economic Buyer. ABM campaigns with account-custom messaging. Highest-investment content (custom decks, technical deep-dives, bespoke TCO). |
| Priority 2 | 60 to 79 | Focused positioning. Lead with two to three pillars matched to the account's sub-sector. Standard proof points plus light account customisation. LinkedIn and direct email marketing. Template-based content. |
| Priority 3 | 40 to 59 | Sub-sector positioning. No proactive outbound marketing. Messaging deployed on inbound with medium urgency. Standard content only, no customisation. |
| Disqualified | Below 40, any gating DQ, or anti-profile | No proactive activity. Inbound requests receive polite decline or deferral with brief reference to ICP v0.3 fit criteria. Record maintained in the Qualification Matrix against re-qualification. |

### 14.2 Pillar-to-tier weighting

Not every pillar applies with the same weight at every tier. The table below is guidance, not a rule: sales and marketing should judge based on the specific account context.

| Pillar | Primary at Tier 1 | Primary at Tier 2 | Primary at Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pillar 1: Launch faster | Digital Banking, Lending, FX, BaaS | Same, with lighter custom evidence | Digital Banking only |
| Pillar 2: Focus on customer value | Universal | Universal (lighter) | Secondary |
| Pillar 3: Experiences that delight | Digital Banking, Retail | Digital Banking only | Secondary |
