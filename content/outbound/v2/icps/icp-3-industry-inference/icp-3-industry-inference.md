### ICP Definition: Industry Inference

**1. ICP Archetype Name:** `Card-Adjacent Unaware - The Peer-Shadowed Operator`

**2. Core Thesis:** A company with no card program, in a vertical where peers already run card programs for a use case that would work here. They may never have considered a card, or may only be starting to think about one, so the case for them comes from their vertical more than from anything they have said. This is a two-stage sale: first why a card program at all, carried by what card programs have done in their vertical, and only then why TXN. Their single dominant pain is **falling behind**, and they may not have noticed it yet.

**3. Rubric-Aligned Profile**

- **Tier 1: Specific Business Type (50% Weight)**
    - **Primary Industry:** `Financial Services`, `Retail`, `E-commerce`
    - **Ideal Verticals:** `SMB lending`, `Consumer lending`, `FX payments`, `Investment management and trading apps`, `Disbursement and payouts`, `Payments`, `Banking as a service (BaaS)`, `Retail`, `E-commerce and marketplaces`.
    - **Defining Characteristics & Offerings (The "Idiot's Guide"):**
        - **Core Identity:** A company in a card-adjacent vertical, with no card that customers can order or use, that holds a direct customer relationship and already moves money to or from those customers.
        - **Website Evidence:** No live card. What is present instead: a customer account or login; a stored balance, wallet, store credit or loyalty scheme; a payout or disbursement flow; a subscription or recurring billing relationship.
    - **State Modifiers (Observable Signals of this Archetype):**
        - **GTM Motion:** Investment in their own customer relationship: the careers page lists customer, loyalty, retention or product roles.
        - **Marketing Motion:** Loyalty, rewards, membership or wallet propositions on the website.
        - **Growth Signal:** A funding announcement or an expansion that grows the customer base a card would serve.
- **Tier 2: Business Model Category (30% Weight)**
    - **Primary Model:** Any model with a recurring or account-based customer relationship: `Marketplace`, `Subscription`, or a platform with a payout side.
    - **Target Customer:** `Consumers` or `SMBs`, held as accounts rather than as one-off transactions. A descriptor, not a qualifier.
    - **Tech Stack Signal:** An acquirer for payments in, with nothing for payments out. A loyalty or rewards platform. A customer identity or account system.
- **Tier 3: Firmographics (20% Weight)**
    - **Employee Count (Range):** `Any`. Headcount does not rank this ICP: a company of any size is in scope, including companies with fewer than 10 people.
    - **Geography (Ideal):** `EEA` and `United Kingdom`, with `Poland`, `Czech Republic`, `Romania` and `Hungary` first.

**4. Disqualification Criteria (Anti-Signals)**

- **Customers can already order or use a card from the company (a live card):** a card page with an order or sign-up flow, live cardholder terms that name an issuer, a usable card in the app store listing, or a help page about using or replacing the card. Card art, a waitlist, "coming soon" or "beta" on its own is not a live card. The company is then a new-program, full-switch or static case.
- **A card-essential vertical** (digital banking, expense management, crypto with fiat rails): a company there with no card is a first-program case, which ranks higher.
- **The company sells only wholesale, or business-to-business-to-business with no end customer:** there is nobody to give a card to.
- **The business moves no money to or from its customers,** for example a pure software licence: there is no use case for a card.
- **A card program for the company's own staff only:** outside TXN's sub-sectors.
- **A BIN sponsor:** a bank or e-money institution that issues cards for other companies' programs under its own licence. TXN works with BIN sponsors directly, as a separate channel, outside outreach.
- **The company's customers are mainly in Asia or Ukraine:** a card for them would serve markets TXN does not support.
- **Petrol retail, a government body, a state-owned company, or a Tier 1 bank itself:** outside TXN's scope.
- **In administration, insolvency or wind-down, or reported for regulator difficulty or sanctions:** the company cannot commit to a launch.

**5. Archetype-to-Pain Mapping (Sharpened for FALLING BEHIND)**
Connects observable signals to the gap a company has not noticed yet: peers in its own vertical already turned the same customer relationship into a card.

| Observable Signal (from Tiers 1 & 2) | Primary Inferred Pain (The Core Problem) | Relevance to the Offer (Why We Win) |
|---|---|---|
| A customer account, and money moving to or from customers, in one of the ideal verticals, where card programs already exist | **`Their sector has turned the same customer relationship into a card, and they have not.`** Companies in their vertical already give customers a card for the balance, the payouts or the spend that this company handles. The gap is visible to their market before it is visible to them. | TXN is the platform for any company launching and operating a card program, and it lets a company run the program without employing card experts. |
| A stored balance, wallet, store credit or loyalty scheme, with no card | **`Money already sits with them and cannot be spent anywhere else.`** Customers hold a balance that only works inside the business. The relationship earns less than it could, and the balance sheet shows it. | TXN can authorise card spend against a balance it holds on behalf of the client, or route each decision to the client to make. Spend and merchant controls decide where that balance can be spent. |
| A payout or disbursement flow to customers or suppliers, paid by bank transfer | **`Every payout is a cost and a delay, not a product.`** They move money out without owning the moment it is spent. Each transfer is an operations cost with nothing to show for it in the customer relationship. | Payouts can go onto a card the company issues itself. Virtual and physical cards are issued and managed across their whole life, with controls on where they can be spent. |
| A subscription or account-based relationship, with no payment instrument of their own | **`They own the customer and rent the payment.`** The most valuable part of the relationship, the moment of spend, belongs to someone else. They know the customer, but not how the customer spends. | The core job of TXN is to let a company run a card program without employing card experts. Guided setup and an assistant in the Console carry the knowledge, because nobody in this company runs a card program today. |
| Investment in loyalty, rewards or membership, with no card | **`They are buying engagement the hard way.`** They already spend money to keep customers close, through points and perks. The instrument they chose sits outside the spend itself. | Spend and merchant controls by merchant category, geography and transaction type decide where the card works. Every card event is reachable through the API, events and webhooks, so their own loyalty systems can act on it. |
