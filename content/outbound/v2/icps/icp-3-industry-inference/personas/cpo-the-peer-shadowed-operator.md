### CPO, Head of Product or Head of Customer (Champion, co-leads)

**1.0 Archetype Name** The Peer-Shadowed Operator

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**
- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the customer relationship and the product experience at a company that holds accounts and moves money, and has never thought of itself as being in payments.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
  - Owning engagement and retention across a customer base held as accounts rather than as one-off transactions.
  - Running what the company has instead of a card: a stored balance, a wallet, store credit, a loyalty scheme or a payout flow.
  - Deciding where the next increment of customer value comes from, on a roadmap where every option is incremental.
  - Watching the competitive set, and being the person expected to notice when it moves.
  - Building the case for anything new, in an organisation with no history of doing this.
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** Scope: department to business. Seniority: senior leadership to executive. Decision Authority: owns the product roadmap and the customer proposition; does not hold budget for a new line, and cannot commission this alone.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**
- **Identity & Self-Image:** The person who understands the customer better than anyone else in the building, and who is quietly frustrated at how small the levers they hold are.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Depth of relationship. They are trying to own more of the customer's life, not more of the customer's clicks.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** By retention, and by whether customers do more with them this year than last.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Does not think of themselves as a payments person, and is slightly intimidated by the label; payments vocabulary they would have to look up loses them. Assumes a card is a banking product that needs a licence, a bank and a department they do not have, and has never checked whether that is still true.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
Someone sends them a screenshot of a peer's new card. Their first reaction is that it is a gimmick, and their second, not long after, is that it is not. The peer took the position this company is already in, with the customers, their balances and the payouts that go to them, and made it into a product, and nobody here thought of it. Most of the week is the usual work: retention numbers that move slowly, and a loyalty scheme that gets a modest response. When they raise the card internally, the reply is polite and immediate: are we allowed to do that, do we need a licence, is that not a bank thing? They have no answers, and that, more than any decision against it, is why the idea has not moved.

**5.0 Pains & Fears (Expanded Dossier)**
- **5.1 Operational Pains (Daily Fires):**
  - **"We pay our customers by bank transfer, and that is where the relationship stops."** We hand them money and see nothing of what happens to it afterwards.
  - **"We are buying engagement with the weakest instrument available, and we know it."** Points and perks get a modest response.
  - **"Customers hold balances with us and cannot spend them anywhere."** The money already sits with us, and we have given it nowhere to go.
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
  - **"A competitor built a line of business out of the position we are already in."** It is visible to our market. It may already be visible to our customers.
  - **"Nobody here can tell me whether a company like ours is allowed to do this, so the idea never advances."** The blocker is not a decision. It is that nobody can answer the first question.
  - **"Every idea I have is an improvement, and none of them is an addition."** I am optimising a proposition rather than extending it, and I can feel the ceiling.
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That the peer's card becomes a normal expectation in this vertical, the company has to explain why it has none, and the honest answer is that nobody could say whether it was possible, so it never got a meeting. Not being beaten: being late for an unglamorous reason.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**
- **Built or bought a loyalty and rewards program:** it did not touch the moment the customer actually spends.
- **Improved the payout experience, with faster transfers, better notifications and a nicer screen:** it made payouts slightly less annoying and changed nothing about the relationship.
- **Looked into a card through a bank:** the proposition on offer was somebody else's card with their logo on it, not a product of their own.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**
- **What they weigh evaluating this category:**
  - Whether a card program makes sense for a company like theirs at all. This comes before any question about a vendor.
  - Whether it is possible for them, and what it would ask of them: a licence, a bank, or neither.
  - Whether it changes the customer relationship enough to be worth being first internally.
  - Whether their current team could operate it without card knowledge they do not have.
- **Objections / sources of resistance:**
  - "Are we even allowed to do this? Do we need to be a bank?"
  - "We have never done anything like this, and we have nobody who has."
  - "This is not on the roadmap, and I do not have a budget line to put it in."
  - "Show me what a card would do for a business like ours, not what your platform can do."

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A named peer in the same vertical launching a card program, which puts the idea in front of them.
- Annual planning, the only moment a new line can be proposed.
- Funding or expansion that grows the customer base a card would serve, and with it the size of the prize.

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Their own vertical's trade press and events, where the peer's launch is discussed and where this population pays attention.
- Product leaders at peers in the same vertical, trusted because their view is not a payments view.
- Operator communities in their vertical, rather than fintech ones, which they do not read.
- Their own customer research and support conversations, where the complaint about unspent balances already sits.

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From payouts as a cost and a delay, to a payout the customer keeps using.
- From balances sitting unspent, to balances that can be spent, with controls attached.
- From buying engagement through a rewards scheme, to engagement tied to where the money actually goes.
- From a proposition they can only improve, to one they have extended.

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Open with what card programs have done for companies in their vertical, without naming a company, so the first case is for a card program at all and TXN comes second. Then answer the question that stops the idea inside the company: whether they are allowed. The claim is that TXN is the platform for any company launching and operating a card program, so the company does not have to be a bank, and it can run the program without employing card experts.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Chief Product Officer · Head of Product · Head of Customer Experience · Director of Loyalty and Membership · Head of Digital · General Manager, Consumer
