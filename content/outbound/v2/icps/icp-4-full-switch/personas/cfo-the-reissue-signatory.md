### CFO or Finance Director (Economic Buyer, holds the reissue bill)

**1.0 Archetype Name** The Reissue Signatory

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**
- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the one number in this deal that is real rather than modelled: what it costs to put a new card in the hand of every cardholder.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
  - Sizing the migration cost, of which the reissue is the dominant and least avoidable part.
  - Weighing that one-off against the ongoing saving, and setting how long it takes to pay back.
  - Owning the exit from the current contract: notice, term, early termination and what is still owed.
  - Funding the period when the company pays for two platforms at once.
  - Answering for a large, visible one-off cost that produces no new revenue.
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** Scope: business. Seniority: executive. Decision Authority: the final commercial signature; owns the exit negotiation; holds a commercial veto that can postpone the deal.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**
- **Identity & Self-Image:** The person who insists the full cost is on the table before anyone commits, and who has been proved right often enough to be unembarrassed about it.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Completeness of the number. A cheap decision with a missing line is worse than an expensive one that is fully stated.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** By whether the actual cost matched the approved cost. Payback discipline is the professional standard they hold themselves to.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Believes the ongoing saving is real, and knows the one-off is what stops these projects.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The saving is not the hard part: they believe the run rate improves, and they could defend the argument. The hard part is a one-off that buys nothing new: a new card for every cardholder, to arrive at what looks from outside like the same place with a different supplier. Then there is the period of paying for both platforms at once. Then the exit: notice, term and whatever is still owed. They asked for a payback period this morning, got a range, and sent it back. The number has to be complete before it goes to the board, not after.

**5.0 Pains & Fears (Expanded Dossier)**
- **5.1 Operational Pains (Daily Fires):**
  - **"The reissue is a large cash cost that buys nothing new."** Plastic, branding, personalisation, production with the chip, and postage, across every card we have.
  - **"There is a period where we pay two platforms, and it is not in anybody's model."** Certain, short, and always missing.
  - **"Every business case I get leads with the run rate and buries the transition."** I keep sending them back, and they keep coming back the same way.
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
  - **"Approving this makes it mine at every board meeting until it is done."** A visible one-off cost has an owner in a way an ongoing saving never does.
  - **"The exit is a negotiation, and the other side holds the notice period."** Once we give notice, they know we are leaving.
  - **"If activation drops after the reissue, the saving shrinks and the cost does not."** The downside is lopsided, and it lands after I have signed.
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That they approve a migration on a payback case, the transition costs run over, the ongoing saving arrives later and smaller than modelled, and the company spends years on a platform that is better in ways nobody can see and was expensive in ways everyone remembers. Not the cost: being the person who approved a number that turned out to be wrong.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**
- **Used the threat of moving as a bargaining position at the last renewal:** it settled the price but not the reasons that made them raise it, and those reasons are still there.
- **Asked for a business case that includes the full transition cost:** each one underweighted the reissue, so they now build the reissue line themselves.
- **Explored running to expiry to avoid the reissue cost:** cheaper in cash and longer in time, which means paying two platforms for years rather than months, and the arithmetic did not clearly favour it.
- **Asked what leaving costs under the current contract:** the answer was accurate, and it showed that the exit is a negotiation rather than a clause.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**
- **What they weigh evaluating this category:**
  - The total transition cost, with the reissue, the period of paying both platforms and the internal effort all stated, before anything else is discussed.
  - The payback period on the ongoing saving, as a number rather than a range.
  - Term, minimums and notice on the new arrangement, since they are about to be reminded what those cost.
  - Whether unit costs improve with volume, which is the only way the saving grows rather than erodes.
- **Objections / sources of resistance:**
  - "The reissue cost is the whole conversation. Everything else is a rounding error against it."
  - "You will not give me numbers, and I am pricing a migration."
  - "What is the total cost of ownership over three years, including implementation, migration and ongoing change requests?"
  - "What are your term and notice? I have just learned what those are worth."

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- The renewal or notice date on the current contract: the only window where the exit cost is bounded.
- An acquisition or a funding round, which both triggers a platform review and funds the transition.
- A quarter with the balance-sheet room to absorb a one-off, which is what decides the timing.
- A price increase from the current processor, which changes the cost of staying.

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Finance peers who have funded a processor migration, for the transition numbers that no vendor and no case study provides.
- Their own invoices and card base data: the only fully trusted evidence in the room.
- Card manufacturing and fulfilment quotes, obtained directly, because that is the largest line and they will not take it from a platform vendor.
- Board members with payments experience, whose view on timing carries more weight than any analysis.

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From a business case that leads with the run rate, to one that states the transition cost first and in full.
- From a payback expressed as a range, to a payback date they will stand behind.
- From paying two platforms as an unmodelled surprise, to a bounded, funded period with an end.

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Open on the transition cost, which this buyer rarely hears first: the reissue is the dominant line. Stating it before they ask earns more credibility than any capability claim.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Chief Financial Officer · Finance Director · VP Finance · Head of Commercial Finance · Group Financial Controller · Chief Operating Officer, finance-owning
