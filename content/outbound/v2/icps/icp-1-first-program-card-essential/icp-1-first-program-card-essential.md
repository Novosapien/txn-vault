### ICP Definition: First Program, Card Is Essential

**1. ICP Archetype Name:** `First-Program Card-Essential - The Committed Newcomer`

**2. Core Thesis:** A fintech building a product that does not work without a card, with no card that customers can use yet. The decision to have a card program was made when they chose what to build, so the open question is who they launch with. They have no current processor to compare against and no card spend to model, and often nobody in the building has run a card program before, so they do not know what they do not know. Because the card is core to the business, the choice feels too important to get wrong, and the selection can stall for far longer than it should. Their single dominant pain is **fear of getting the decision wrong**.

**3. Rubric-Aligned Profile**

- **Tier 1: Specific Business Type (50% Weight)**
    - **Primary Industry:** `FinTech`, `Financial Services`, `Banking`, `Retail`, `E-commerce`
    - **Ideal Verticals:** `Digital banking for consumers (neobanks)`, `Digital banking for businesses (SME accounts)`, `Crypto platforms with fiat rails`, `Expense management and corporate cards`, `Travel and expense`, `Accounts payable`, `SMB lending`, `Consumer lending`, `FX payments and multi-currency accounts`, `Payments`, `Banking as a service (BaaS)`, `Investment management and trading apps`, `Disbursement and payouts`, `Retail`, `E-commerce and marketplaces`. Every vertical carries this ICP. The Core Identity decides the fit, not the vertical, so some verticals hold no company of this kind.
    - **Defining Characteristics & Offerings (The "Idiot's Guide"):**
        - **Core Identity:** A company whose product needs a card to work (an account, a spend platform, or crypto converted into money to spend), and that has no card customers can order or use today. The test: if the product went without a card, would it look incomplete?
        - **Website Evidence:** A card, wallet or spend feature described in the product pages, with no card that customers can order yet. "Waitlist", "coming soon" or "beta" language attached to the card. A pricing page that assumes a card. App store screenshots of a card that cannot be ordered yet.
    - **State Modifiers (Observable Signals of this Archetype):**
        - **GTM Motion:** The careers page lists card, payments, issuing or scheme roles while no card is live.
        - **Marketing Motion:** Launch content for the card on the website: waitlist campaigns, "be the first to get your card", founder posts about why they are building it.
        - **Growth Signal:** The website or its news page mentions an e-money or payment institution licence in progress, a funding round that names a card or payments product, or an incubator or accelerator cohort.
- **Tier 2: Business Model Category (30% Weight)**
    - **Primary Model:** `Any`. How the company sells to its own customers does not rank this ICP.
    - **Target Customer:** `Consumers`, `SMBs` or `Corporates`, by vertical. A descriptor, not a qualifier.
    - **Tech Stack Signal:** The website's integrations or careers pages name a core banking or ledger platform (`Mambu`, `Thought Machine`, `10x`, `Tuum`) and no card issuer processor, or name a KYC vendor (`Onfido`, `Sumsub`, `Veriff`).
- **Tier 3: Firmographics (20% Weight)**
    - **Employee Count (Range):** `Any`. Headcount does not rank this ICP: a company of any size is in scope, including companies with fewer than 10 people.
    - **Geography (Ideal):** `EEA` and `United Kingdom`, with `Poland`, `Czech Republic`, `Romania` and `Hungary` first. TXN contracts only with companies in Europe (Switzerland included) or the UK. Where the cards may be issued is the BIN sponsor's decision, not TXN's. A company that publishes that its card will be issued for markets outside Europe and the UK (for example Asia, the Middle East or Africa), or for Ukraine, ranks lower here. It is not disqualified. Do not try to identify the licensed issuer.

**4. Disqualification Criteria (Anti-Signals)**

- **Customers can already order or use the card (a live card):** a card page with an order or sign-up flow, live cardholder terms that name an issuer, a usable card in the app store listing, or a help page about using or replacing the card. Card art, a waitlist, "coming soon" or "beta" on its own is not a live card. The company is then a new-program, full-switch or static case.
- **The product is not an account, a spend platform or crypto converted into money to spend:** for example a loan, an investment or trading app, a payout service or a shop. A card would not look missing from it. The company is then an industry-inference case.
- **Crypto only, with no fiat rails:** there is no money to spend, so there is no card use case.
- **A card program for the company's own staff only:** outside TXN's sub-sectors.
- **A BIN sponsor:** a bank or e-money institution that issues cards for other companies' programs under its own licence. TXN works with BIN sponsors directly, as a separate channel, outside outreach.
- **No company in Europe or the UK to contract with:** the website shows the company and its group registered only outside Europe and the UK. TXN can contract only with companies in Europe or the UK. A European or UK subsidiary of a group based elsewhere is not disqualified, and a website that does not say where the company is registered is not evidence.
- **Petrol retail, a government body, a state-owned company, or a Tier 1 bank itself:** outside TXN's scope. A Tier 1 bank's digital subsidiary is not disqualified here.
- **A traditional bank (a retail, savings, cooperative or regional bank), not a digital bank or neobank:** a low priority for later. TXN needs reference clients and credibility before it can win banks. The company stays on record and is revisited later.
- **In administration, insolvency or wind-down, or reported for regulator difficulty or sanctions:** the company cannot commit to a launch.

**5. Archetype-to-Pain Mapping (Sharpened for FEAR OF GETTING THE DECISION WRONG)**
Connects observable signals to the fear of a company that must choose a processor for its first card program, with no experience to judge the choice by.

| Observable Signal (from Tiers 1 & 2) | Primary Inferred Pain (The Core Problem) | Relevance to the Offer (Why We Win) |
|---|---|---|
| The website names a core banking or ledger platform, and no card processor | **`They have built the account and cannot judge who should build the card.`** The product is visibly incomplete, and the missing piece is one that few people in the building have done before. They do not know which questions decide the choice, so every option looks as risky as the next. | TXN sets up the card program as configuration rather than bespoke code. The specialist knowledge of running a card program sits in the platform, so the decision to launch does not need a card expert on the payroll. |
| The card is on a waitlist or "coming soon", and customers cannot order it | **`They have announced a card and have not chosen who will run it.`** The launch is public, and the choice behind it is still open because it feels too important to get wrong. Each week spent in selection eats into a date that customers can already see. | TXN targets under ten business days from contract signature to the first live transaction. After launch, a change to the card product is a setting, not a new release. |
| Card, payments or issuing roles on the careers page, with no card live | **`They are hiring someone to make the decision safe.`** Until a specialist arrives, nobody in the building can test a vendor's answers, so the choice waits on a hire. Then the knowledge sits in one person, and it leaves when they do. | The core job of TXN is to let a company run a card program without employing card experts. Guided setup, an assistant in the Console and documentation generated from the API carry knowledge that would otherwise sit in one person. |
| An e-money or payment institution licence in progress, and no processor named | **`The licence will arrive before the decision does.`** A regulatory clock is running against a choice nobody feels ready to make. When the licence lands, the business case expects cards soon after. | Scheme and regulatory requirements arrive as delivered outcomes. PSD2 strong customer authentication and 3DS sit inside the platform, and clearing and settlement are reconciled in the Console. |
| An incubator or accelerator cohort, with the card named in the proposition | **`They do not know what they do not know about card programs.`** They have not chosen a vendor, and they do not yet know which questions matter. They will learn the domain from whichever vendor reaches them first. | Their engineers can integrate through the API and a sandbox that behaves like production, with no sales conversation in the way. Program changes later are settings in the Console, not a new project. |
