### CPO or Head of Product (Champion, leads the deal)

**1.0 Archetype Name** The Promised Launcher

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**
- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the product end to end at a company whose product does not work without a card, and owns the public promise that it will launch.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
  - Defining what the card does for the customer: who holds it, what it can be spent on, and what it is limited to.
  - Sequencing the launch so that the account, the app, the onboarding and the card arrive together, because none of them is a product on its own.
  - Choosing the platform in practice, because the decision is a product decision before it is a technical one.
  - Holding the roadmap against a founder and a board who see the card as a feature rather than as infrastructure.
  - Facing the waitlist and the early customers who were given a date.
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** Scope: business. Seniority: executive or senior leadership, often a founding team member. Decision Authority: shapes and effectively selects the vendor and holds the roadmap; does not usually sign the budget, and cannot override the CTO's technical veto.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**
- **Identity & Self-Image:** A builder who ships, measured against the gap between the pitch and what a customer can actually do.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Momentum, and a product that holds together. They would rather ship a narrower thing that works than a wider thing that waits.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Whether the launch date they gave is the date that happens, and whether the product does what the pitch said.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Wary of infrastructure vendors as a group, because they all describe themselves the same way and none of them says what will be different after launch.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
Their roadmap shows the account, the funding, the app and the transaction feed, and then a box marked "card" with an unchosen vendor behind it. Most of the day goes on other work that turns out to depend on that box. A routine question from engineering, such as who decides when an authorisation is declined, can stop a meeting, because nobody in the room is sure whether it is the company's decision or the platform's. Vendors all promise a fast launch in the same words, and they have no way to test those words. The waitlist grows, and the date they gave is already public.

**5.0 Pains & Fears (Expanded Dossier)**
- **5.1 Operational Pains (Daily Fires):**
  - **"Nobody gives me a straight answer on how long this takes."** Every vendor gives a range anchored on a best case, and I have nothing to hold them to.
  - **"Every question about the card opens three more."** I asked about cards used abroad and got scheme rules, interchange and routing. I do not know which of those are my problem.
  - **"My team is estimating work in a domain they have not worked in."** The estimate is a guess, and my roadmap depends on it.
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
  - **"I gave a date before I knew what sat behind it."** The waitlist and the board both have it.
  - **"If I choose wrong, I find out in six months, and by then it is the whole product."** The card platform sits underneath everything we sell.
  - **"I make this call, and product has the least card knowledge of anyone who will touch it."**
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That the launch slips for a reason they were warned about in a sentence they did not understand, and the company's credibility with its customers and board does not recover.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**
- **Read the vendors' public material:** it showed what is possible, not what is normal or what goes wrong.
- **Ran a feature comparison:** every vendor ticked nearly every box, and the differences that matter did not appear on it.
- **Scoped building it in-house:** dropped once scheme certification and the ongoing scheme mandates came up, without certainty that dropping it was right.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**
- **What they weigh evaluating this category:**
  - Whether the product can change after launch without a new integration, because the first version will be wrong.
  - Whether their own team can run it without first hiring a card specialist.
  - How much of the scheme and regulatory work lands on them rather than on the platform.
- **Objections / sources of resistance:**
  - "What is your track record with products like ours? Show me references in our vertical."
  - "How much engineering effort is the first launch, and how much is the second product?"
  - "What happens when I want to change the product in month three?"

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A launch date going into a board pack, a funding announcement or a public waitlist.
- The core banking or ledger platform going live, which leaves the card as the only unfinished piece.
- An e-money or payment institution licence progressing, which starts a regulatory clock.

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Founders and product leads who have already launched a card program, reached directly.
- Their accelerator or investor network, where vendor recommendations travel.
- Fintech newsletters and operator communities, read for patterns rather than vendors.

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From a launch date they defend nervously, to one they state plainly because they can explain what sits behind it.
- From a product with a gap where the card should be, to a customer completing the whole journey in one session.
- From a first version they are stuck with, to changing the card product in configuration when the first version turns out wrong.

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Lead on the date they have already made public and the missing piece behind it: the account is built and the card is not. The claim that cuts through is that they can run the card program without employing card experts, and change it after launch as configuration rather than a new project.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Chief Product Officer · Head of Product · VP Product · Founder and CPO · Head of Card Products
