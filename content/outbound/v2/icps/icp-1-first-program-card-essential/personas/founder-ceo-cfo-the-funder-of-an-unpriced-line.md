### Founder, CEO or CFO (Economic Buyer, funds it)

**1.0 Archetype Name** The Funder of an Unpriced Line

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**
- **2.1 Primary Function:** Holds the money and the runway, and must commit to a platform cost with no comparable line in the company's history.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
  - Modelling what the card program costs at the planned volumes, and at the volumes that will actually arrive.
  - Deciding what gets funded this quarter against a finite runway.
  - Answering to a board or investors for a vendor commitment in a category they will not question closely.
  - Judging whether the company is buying a platform or taking on a permanent dependency.
  - In a small company, doing all of this while also being the founder who chose to build the product.
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** Scope: business. Seniority: executive or founder. Decision Authority: the final commercial signature; owns the budget; can veto on cost and term.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**
- **Identity & Self-Image:** The person who keeps the company alive, thinking in months of runway.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Optionality first, predictability second. They would pay more for a commitment they can leave than less for one they cannot.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Whether the company can still choose. A decision that removes future choices is expensive at any price.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Assumes infrastructure pricing is built to be hard to model, and that the headline number is not the real one.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
They are putting a number into a model for something the company has never bought. Every other line has a comparable, such as salaries, cloud and compliance; this one is built from a vendor's own description of its own pricing. The board will ask what the card program costs at ten times the plan, and the honest answer is a range too wide to be useful. Product says the date matters more than the price, engineering says not to rush, and they are the one who has to choose which risk to take. The choice has already taken longer than planned, because nobody in the company has made it before, and every month it stays open is runway spent on a product that cannot launch.

**5.0 Pains & Fears (Expanded Dossier)**
- **5.1 Operational Pains (Daily Fires):**
  - **"I am modelling a cost line with no comparable and no numbers."** I have a structure described to me and no figures, so my model is a shape, not a forecast.
  - **"Nobody can tell me what this costs at scale, and scale is the point."** If the unit economics only work at a volume we have not reached, I need to know now.
  - **"Every question about money gets answered with value."**
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
  - **"This is a dependency, not a purchase."** Once cardholders sit on someone's platform, leaving has a price I cannot see yet.
  - **"I am the only real check on this decision."** Nobody on the board will catch it if I get it wrong.
  - **"The people telling me it is fine are the people who want it."** Product wants the date, engineering wants the clean integration, and neither of them signs.
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** Being locked into a platform whose cost grows faster than revenue, where leaving means reissuing every card and rebuilding the integration. Not overspending: losing the ability to choose.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**
- **Asked vendors for pricing:** got a structure from each and figures from none.
- **Built a model on assumed unit costs:** the range was too wide to tell vendors apart, which was the only reason to build it.
- **Kept the vendor selection open until they felt certain:** it ran far longer than planned and brought no certainty, because they could not judge the answers they were given.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**
- **What they weigh evaluating this category:**
  - Whether the cost curve is predictable, and whether unit economics improve with growth.
  - The term, the minimums and the notice, because they set the cost of being wrong.
  - Whether the platform removes a hire they would otherwise need, since salaries are the largest avoidable cost here.
  - Whether the vendor will still be there, and still serving them, in three years.
- **Objections / sources of resistance:**
  - "What is my cost per active card, and how does pricing scale at ten times my volume?"
  - "What are the costs that are not in the headline quote?"
  - "You have no live clients yet. What happens to us if you do not make it?"
  - "What is the real cost of leaving, in cards, engineering time and months?"

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A funding round closing, which turns a plan into a spending mandate with a clock.
- A board meeting with the launch date and its cost on the agenda.
- The runway model being rebuilt, with the card program as its largest unpriced line.

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Their investors and other founders in the same portfolio, where real pricing knowledge travels.
- Finance leads at companies of the same stage, asked privately.
- Little published content: this persona does not read vendor material.

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From an unpriced line in the model, to a forecast they will defend at the board without hedging.
- From needing to hire card expertise, to running the program with the team already funded.
- From a dependency with an unknown exit cost, to a commitment whose term, notice and exit they have read and accepted.

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Open on the cost they can avoid. The program is designed to run without employing card experts, which is the claim with money attached. An optional second point: the pricing structure is simple, which answers their belief that infrastructure pricing is built to be hard to model.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Founder and CEO · Co-Founder · Chief Financial Officer · VP Finance · Finance Director
