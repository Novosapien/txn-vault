## Notes for reviewers

- **Pilot carve-out (22 July):** Ian asked to start the 6-week agentic pilot before his 3-week leave (from Fri 24 July COB), ahead of finalising the MSA. The [[txn-agentic-pilot-order]] is a standalone interim contract for just the pilot: £54,187.50 (1.5 months at the £36,125/mo build rate), 50% on signature / 50% on completion. It is not contingent on the MSA. On MSA signature the pilot becomes the completed **Statement of Work 1**, its charge reconciled in MSA Schedule 2, IP already vested. Consequence: the **MSA's SoW 1 and Schedule 2 need updating** so the build charge reflects the remaining wire-in only (£90,312.50, from £144,500), and the **main agentic-layer proposal is being rebuilt** to show the pilot as done/separate. Top open question: whether the pilot touches real personal data (governs the DP clause).
- **Configuration work is tracked in [[content-workforce]]**, which holds the manifesto, pillars and entity outputs as they are built, plus the workshop schedule and the inputs still owed by TXN.
- **Content Workforce interview schedule locked (18-08, [[2026-08-18-agentic-standup]]):** Wed 19 Aug 10:30 content manifesto and pillars; Thu 20 Aug 11:00 brand entity; Mon 24 Aug outbound interview one, the offer, with George attending as it is the foundational session; Tue 25 Aug 14:00 ICPs; Thu 27 Aug 10:30 personas. Individual interviews follow from the week of 24 Aug, led by **Tyler**, who joins formally on 10 September and is already picking up customers. Max has drafted the interview templates from the vault and is reviewing them with Tyler, so the sessions start from a draft rather than a blank page.
- **Dorte's priority, stated plainly (18-08):** the console matters less than lead generation. *"The console is not as important as such because we need to have first customers to use the console... the better we get the content workforce and the lead generation running, then we will see if we get a customer through the door sooner."* Pre-launch September, full launch October or November. Worth weighing when the wider engagement is sequenced.
- **Content Workforce initiated (13-08, [[2026-08-13-content-workforce-initiate]]).** Shape agreed with Dorte: **four entities** (the TXN business, Ian, Michael and Dorte), with individual profiles included at no charge and a **TXN content pillar** so a personal post can be written in the company's register. **Four interview sets**: one business plus three individual. The business session is budgeted at up to four hours but will be **split into shorter blocks**, since a single four-hour booking will not get Ian and Michael in the room; Max pre-feeds the vault so the session is a **confirmation exercise rather than a blank start**, and estimates the business one could compress to about an hour. The **four-week setup window was a deliberate buffer**, not an estimate. Dorte's sequencing: **SoW signed by end of next week, work starting the week after**, with all pre-work assembled in advance so nothing waits on signature. **Outbound Workforce is deferred** to a later phase. Still owed by TXN: **website content and messaging from Bronwyn** (Ian was leading it and it has not been shared), **LinkedIn logins**, and profiles brought up to standard.
- **Content Workforce — TXN said yes (04-08, [[2026-08-04-content-workforce-demo-paycorp]]).** After her own demo, Dorte confirmed on the call: *"we really like the solution, so we want to go with it."* **Ian is on leave**, so nothing is signed; the GTM Workforces proposal needs to come back to him on his return. Dorte's own framing is that TXN is easy to configure — *"we're just three people"* — compared with a group rollout.
- **Pricing quoted verbally on that call**, in front of a third party (Pay Corp's CMO), and **should be reconciled against the proposal before anything is papered**: a **tiered fee of ~£1,750**, a **setup fee of ~£3,000** covering one-to-two weeks of interviewing the exec team, and **consumption "all the way down to seven pounds" per piece of content** at scale. Both models offered — pure consumption, or a committed tier for spend control. _Figures as spoken from memory on the call; treat the proposal as authoritative._
- **Pay Corp is a prospect, not a commitment.** Bronwyn Watt (CMO of **Pay Corp**, a shareholder in TXN) attended at Dorte's invitation to give a marketing opinion and to assess it for her own group. Her position: **let TXN run first and use it as the benchmark** — *"why don't you see how TXN goes and use that as your benchmark"* — before any Pay Corp rollout. She would want the proof points before taking it to her CEOs, whose appetite for LinkedIn she described as inconsistent.
- **Pay Corp shape, for when that conversation comes:** a holding company plus about **five brands**, selling **ATMs** (corporate agreements with formal retail, plus informal retail worked on the ground), **back-end card-machine facilitation**, and **restaurant point of sale**. Her read: LinkedIn suits the **thought-leadership** and **POS/hospitality** lines and the card-machine back end, but **not informal-retail ATM sales** — those buyers are not on LinkedIn. She has brand assets ready (tone of voice, messaging pillars, photo library, logo and animation variants), so onboarding input would be straightforward.
- The GTM Workforces proposal is commercially separate from, and a companion to, the Agentic Layer proposal. **The MSA must be structured to cover both engagements.**
- Outbound Workforce pricing is a test-partner structure: £5,000 setup, £1,500/mo run fee, all usage (Claude compute, Apollo lead data, ElevenLabs voice) passed through at billed cost plus an administration fee. Per-lead pricing is agreed after the 90-day review. No per-lead rate is stated in the proposal by design.
- Salary figures on the Content Workforce comparison page are indicative UK market benchmarks, flagged as such on the page.
- All figures exclusive of VAT.
